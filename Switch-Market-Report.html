Energy Market Update

Escalating tensions between the US and Iran, European storage and the outlook for business energy buyers.

Market Update

Energy markets have reacted further to escalating tensions between the US and Iran. Attacks targeting Kharg Island, Iran’s main oil export hub, alongside significant strikes on the IRGC and wider Iranian infrastructure, have added further upside pressure to prices.

This comes alongside continued supply concerns, with European storage levels remaining below the five-year average at 65.39%.

An important factor remains the race to refill storage ahead of 2027. With supply metrics already under pressure, injection rates are likely to remain constrained. In addition, competition for gas has increased, with Asian spot LNG prices reaching a five-month high.

An additional concern is the underlying risk around liquidity. As markets move to higher premiums, fewer transactions are placed, which can result in sharper intraday movements and increased volatility.

Curve View

Prices at the front of the curve continue to hold a significant risk premium as Winter approaches. However, the current delta between the immediate Winter and further-out prices is likely to be short lived, with volatility expected to remain until geopolitical tensions ease alongside an improvement in wider supply metrics.

Our Outlook

Peter LeylandSenior Energy Trader

Despite sentiment drawing prices higher, European storage levels could reach a sufficient injection level of 75% by the end of October. This could provide ample storage throughout a mild Winter period.

LNG transported through the Strait of Hormuz only made up an average of 7% of European LNG supply in 2025 and, because of this, the risk is not predominantly supply-related.

The majority of the growing risk surrounds attacks on energy infrastructure. If this filters into Europe, or US LNG exports reduce, heightened markets may not just be a ‘peak’ but a new market normal.

Risk Approaches

Low Risk Approach

The outcome would be to reduce exposure to volatile market rates, while aiming to obtain a year-on-year commodity saving. Due to this the approach would be to buy out to 2031. There would be a premium in the initial period to secure budget certainty, but would provide certainty moving forwards.

Medium Risk Approach

Capture periods of opportunity further out, for example where summer periods are still presenting £60/ MWh (6 p/kWh) range. We would float the less opportune periods in order to capture falling rates, if geopolitical negotiations improve and supply metrics recover.

Hight Risk Approach

Each month throughout the initial Winter season would be assessed on a bespoke basis. During periods of high volatility, and when prices are at peaks, ‘opening up’ volume or selling back and then purchasing when prices return to more market-reflective rates could result in an improved Weighted Average Price, combined with a hedge into the forward seasons.
