Futures Market Explained

Future delivery is priced today. The delivery period still determines eligibility and customer fit.

What the curve means

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their existing contract ends to see today's future-delivery price.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. Backwardation does not mean wait. It means future delivery is cheaper today.

What the customer should do

Compare the actual contract end date with the relevant future-start season, then confirm supplier window, product eligibility, quote validity, usage profile, standing charges and non-commodity charges.

Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. Waiting is not neutral. Review-now does not mean contract blindly.

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing.

Ceasefire Reality Check

Diplomacy can reduce premium before physical infrastructure is repaired. The supply test remains flows.

Security and deployment

  • Were Tuesday’s three missiles and four drones intercepted without meaningful energy damage?
  • Are Eastern Region oilfields, Ras Tanura, Abqaiq, Dhahran, East-West, Yanbu and Rabigh operating with measured output?
  • Has Saudi Arabia formally requested Egyptian assets, and have any deployed?
  • Have UK, French or Turkish statements become verified operational missions?

Markets and physical supply

  • Have Iran talks produced a signed, enforceable ceasefire or maritime transit arrangement?
  • Do UKMTO and operators confirm repeatable safe LNG and oil passage?
  • Are Qatari loadings rising sustainably, or do US spot cargoes still compensate?
  • Are Norway, US LNG, storage and UK generation offsetting the shortage?
  • Is public gas easing accompanied by lower executable supplier books?

Diplomatic contact is not a ceasefire, and a ceasefire announcement is not verified safe passage.

An intercepted missile is not a confirmed energy infrastructure hit, and a hit is not necessarily lost production.

An improvement in security can reduce a risk premium before damaged infrastructure is repaired, but physical flows are the supply test.

Shipping Risk

Route, vessel, cargo and evidence type matter more than a generic regional headline.

HORMUZ

Restricted and high risk. No fresh, directly comparable current oil-clearance series was established. Historical 18% and 32% comparisons use unresolved definitions and are not charted. LNG requires repeatable safe passage, not an occasional crossing.

Check: UKMTO, Kpler/Vortexa definitions, named LNG departures and insurance.

GULF OF OMAN

Kuwait-flagged product tanker Al Dasma was struck by an unknown projectile 11 nautical miles west of Bukha on 21 October. The fire was extinguished, crew were safe and the vessel anchored. This was not an overnight 27 October incident and was not an LNG carrier.

PERSIAN GULF

Iranian claims about multiple vessels remain claims unless matched to UKMTO, operators, flag states or trackers. A separate tanker incident southeast of Al Faw on 20 October was reported without damage or environmental impact. No seven-vessel loss count is adopted.

GULF OF ADEN

The 19 October Shabwa attack occurred in a province containing Yemen's Balhaf LNG complex, increasing its energy-security relevance without proving direct LNG infrastructure damage. Balhaf has long been mothballed, so a security incident is not a new supply loss.

BAB AL-MANDEB

Houthi capability and fighting near the Red Sea coast keep the route under review. No fresh 27 October LNG-carrier interruption was verified. Egypt’s defensive authorisation has potential maritime relevance but does not itself change UK gas supply.

RED SEA

As Hormuz becomes less reliable, the Red Sea route becomes more valuable and more strategic. Security, insurance and repeated commercial passage must be assessed together. The security risk zone is not confined to Hormuz.

YANBU / RED SEA CORRIDOR

Yanbu and the East-West pipeline matter because they help Saudi crude bypass Hormuz. Recent Houthi claims are not treated as fresh shutdown proof. Check pumping, terminal loading and refinery operations independently.

LNG CARRIERS

No named fresh LNG-carrier hit was verified in the primary window. For any incident, identify vessel, IMO, laden/ballast state, origin, destination, cargo owner and whether passage actually stopped. An LNG security incident matters more to UK gas than an unrelated general-cargo incident.

TANKER WAR-RISK INSURANCE

A route can remain open and still carry a substantial war-risk premium. Friction gets priced. Freight and insurance can raise delivered cost even where physical oil finds a workaround; that is different from a verified production loss.

No consolidated current vessel-incident count is published because source categories, duplicate aftermath reports and attack-versus-loss dates could not be reconciled reliably in this run.

Qatar needs predictable safe passage, not occasional successful crossings. Oil has more rerouting options than LNG, but only current verified flows establish the net market effect. LNG remains the bridge between Gulf security and UK gas.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

WHOLESALE HAS MOVED. HAVE SUPPLIER BOOKS CAUGHT UP?

The opportunity

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. A valid existing quote can therefore remain below a later replacement-cost view for a limited period.

The new Tuesday internal curve shows that offered indications have now moved higher. Review any still-valid quote immediately rather than assuming it remains available.

The danger

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.

The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow. A public wholesale pullback does not automatically reverse a supplier book that has already repriced.

Switch365 internal commercial experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, some supplier books were withdrawn, suspended or returned higher. This is internal experience, not an official market trigger.
