Route Risk Active Again

Middle East Energy Risk Update - 12 August 2026

UK Gas, UK Power, Futures Market and Procurement Position
Route risk is active again. Hormuz traffic has fallen to a one-week low. Futures Market Explained
How forward pricing works for business energy procurement

The futures market shows the price for energy delivered in future periods. A customer renewing from 2027, 2028, 2029 or 2030 is not only interested in today's price. They should look at what the market is currently offering for their actual delivery period.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period. Waiting lets the market make the decision for the customer. As time passes, that future period becomes the near-term market and can absorb the same risk premium.

The 1 Year Ago line is context. The latest line is the customer's decision point. Backwardation does not mean wait. It means the cheaper part of the curve may be available now, and waiting gives the market more time to take it away.

British Gas 18-Month Window
Verified internal evidence - extended renewal eligibility

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier chance to review future-start pricing. The 18-month British Gas window matters because it turns the forward market into a practical customer action.

Your new contract would not start today. It would begin when your current agreement ends. Waiting is not neutral. If the market moves or British Gas withdraws rates, customers cannot go back and secure today's pricing later. Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. It means check whether the future period can be secured now.

This is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market. If you are a British Gas business customer with a contract ending within 18 months, you can request a renewal quote now and lock in a future-start rate while the backwardation gap is still available.

Supplier Price-Book Lag
The opportunity and the danger

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. Waiting for certainty can mean missing the price movement. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and buy yesterday's price.

Even if oil has fallen from the highs, supplier books may still react to the move that has already happened. Suppliers do not need a full closure of Hormuz or Bab el-Mandeb before they reprice. Suppliers also do not need diplomacy to fail completely before they price renewed conflict risk. When conflict spreads geographically, suppliers can price the increased probability of disruption before physical closure happens.

Hormuz traffic at a one-week low, fatal Bab el-Mandeb shipping risk, Gulf of Oman blockade enforcement and EIA disruption-through-2027 forecasts are all supplier-pricing risk signals. 1 Year Ago prices are useful context, but they do not protect the customer today. The market has already moved up from 1 Year Ago across most periods. If supplier books have not yet fully caught up, that is the window. If they have already repriced, the opportunity is gone.
