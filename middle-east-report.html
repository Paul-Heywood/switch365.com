Today's Commercial Position

The freshest verified physical evidence is a recovery in actual crude export volume. Kpler estimates September crude exports from the main Middle Eastern producers at 12.8 million barrels per day, the highest since the US-Israeli war with Iran began in February, with Hormuz crude flows at approximately 7.4 million bpd and Saudi exports near 5.4 million bpd. Nineteen Saudi VLCCs, each capable of carrying roughly two million barrels, exited the Strait last week. Regional exports remain approximately six million bpd below February's 18.8 million bpd, preliminary AIS-based counts can be revised, and recovering crude volume is not the same as normal Hormuz and LNG operations.

The diplomatic route deteriorated at the same time. Trump rejected Iran's proposed seven-day ceasefire and temporary Hormuz reopening plan on Saturday, although he said on Sunday that he expected further talks during the week. Saudi-led forces said they intercepted two Houthi ballistic missiles heading towards Khamis Mushait and two drones heading towards Riyadh early on Saturday, with no damage reported. The East-West Pipeline restarted at reduced flow on 22 September, but three pumping stations remain damaged and full capacity may take six to eight weeks. Bab al-Mandeb remained physically active over the weekend, with no verified closure or new energy damage.

Oil reacted first. Brent traded at $107.75/bbl, up 3.29%, and WTI at $94.55/bbl, up 2.32%, at 05:40 GMT Monday. European gas did not follow tick for tick: Friday's public NBP and TTF references both fell, UK gas by around 4%, and the early Monday public TTF proxy sat near €73/MWh, still below its mid-September peak. QatarEnergy is still producing very little LNG and 17% of Qatar's capacity remains damaged. The commercial test this morning is the exact live NBP, TTF, UK power and supplier price a customer can actually secure. Brent is not the customer's gas quote.

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their contract end date to see today's price for future delivery: W-26, S-27, W-27 and beyond are all tradable, quotable delivery periods right now.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. The customer mistake is thinking a cheaper future period means they should wait. It does not. It means we should check whether that future period can be secured now. The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period, and waiting gives the market time to remove today's forward-value opportunity.

Backwardation does not mean wait. Waiting is not neutral.

British Gas 18-Month Window

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier chance to review future-start pricing. The 18-month British Gas window matters because it turns the forward market into a practical customer action: with S-27 gas offered 28.2% below W-26 and S-28 offered 53.6% below, customers inside that window can test today's forward value rather than rolling into a renewal at the front of a war-premium curve.

Your new contract would not start today. It would begin when your current agreement ends. Waiting is not neutral: if the market moves or British Gas withdraws rates, customers cannot go back and secure today's pricing later. Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later; it means check whether the future period can be secured now. Not every customer is automatically eligible, so eligibility and live pricing should be confirmed first.

British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action.

European Storage Bullish for price

Storage is a buffer, not immunity: Europe does not need to run out of gas for low storage to increase winter pricing. Confidence: high.

Norwegian pipeline supply remains the primary cushion against missing Gulf LNG. No fresh major Norwegian outage was verified in this research run; September maintenance season is winding down. Strong Norwegian supply materially cushions missing LNG. Confidence: moderate (no fresh Gassco disruption found in this run).

5

US LNG / Replacement LNG Bearish for price

US exports averaged ~17.4 Bcf/d in the first half of 2026, a record pace, with Golden Pass shipping its first cargo in April and Europe taking the large majority of US cargoes. US LNG remains one of the strongest bearish buffers against Qatar disruption. Panama Canal pressure means replacement supply is only a buffer if it can arrive at an acceptable time and cost. Confidence: high.

6

Asian LNG Demand Bearish for price

September Asian LNG imports are forecast at ~20.09 million tonnes, the weakest September since 2018, with China, India and Pakistan reducing buying at crisis-level prices. Asian demand destruction is cushioning Europe, not making LNG cheap. Confidence: high (Reuters / Kpler forecast).

Confidence: internal evidence strong for the offered ranges; book-level changes not verified.

Shipping / Freight / Insurance Bullish for price

War-risk premia, VLCC demand at record levels and Gulf of Oman STS capacity near its practical limits all add friction. Friction gets priced, even where physical volumes recover. Confidence: high (Reuters, Kpler).

The pipeline restarted at reduced flow on 22 September, an oil-side buffer, but full capacity may take six to eight weeks. Bab al-Mandeb remained physically active over the weekend with tracked traffic still depressed near ~26 vessels a day and the Saudi-focused Houthi threat live. Primarily an oil and shipping event rather than a direct UK gas event. Confidence: high.

Trump rejected Iran's seven-day ceasefire and Hormuz reopening proposal on Saturday, then said on Sunday he expected further talks this week. Diplomacy is delayed rather than dead, but the immediate breakthrough failed and duration risk keeps the premium alive. Confidence: high (Reuters, Axios).

Gas still sets the marginal price much of the time, so the power curve follows gas at the front, but generation conditions determine how strongly. Confidence: moderate.

Ceasefire Reality Check

The weekend produced no agreed ceasefire.
