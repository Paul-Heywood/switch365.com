01

Summary

Oil through Hormuz is around 32% below pre-war. Kpler says Hormuz oil clearances averaged 11.62 million barrels per day over the latest reported seven-day period to 13 October, against a pre-war baseline of approximately 17.13 million bpd. That is a volume measurement of oil flow, not a count of ships, and it does not mean Middle East oil supply is down 32%: crude is being bypassed through pipelines, the Red Sea, Fujairah and Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship transfers, and Kpler now judges total non-Iran Middle East crude exports, including diverted volumes, to be running at roughly pre-conflict levels.

The gas picture is significantly worse. Qatar exported only around 1.6 million tonnes of LNG in September, roughly 76% below its pre-war monthly average of about 6.75 million tonnes, and October is currently tracking around 0.6 million tonnes, approximately 91% below pre-war levels if that trajectory holds. The war is hitting LNG far harder than oil. QatarEnergy's force majeure to Edison now runs to early December, with 35 cargoes and roughly 4.6 bcm affected, and Ras Laffan repair timelines are measured in years, not weeks.

For UK businesses the chain that matters is Qatar LNG to TTF to NBP to supplier price books. TTF November settled around €87/MWh on 13 October with funds adding to net long positions, EU storage is 74.0% full and roughly nine points below last year, and Switch365's own Friday morning offered curve moved up across every gas season and almost every power season: W-26 gas is now marked at 210.29p/therm and W-26 power at £161.26/MWh. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. This is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market.

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. Customers do not have to wait until their existing contract expires to see today's price for future delivery.

Today's Switch365 offered curve is in steep backwardation: every later season is priced below the W-26 working reference. Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. Backwardation does not mean wait. It means future delivery is cheaper today. Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity, and today's overnight move shows exactly that: every gas season rose in a single morning, with W-27 up 4.25p/therm. Waiting is not neutral.

Future delivery can currently be priced far below near-term energy where supplier windows allow.

Rhetoric Tracker

Iran

Said: The US response to Iran's seven conditions is "insufficient"; the strait "would not be opened" until they are met (Rezaee, 11 Oct). Officials warned renewed war would put regional infrastructure "within range" and described Hormuz as an Iranian waterway.

Physically happened: Twelve attacks on commercial vessels in the week to 11 October (UKMTO-tracked warnings); IRGC claimed a tanker struck a mine; Iranian crude loadings remain blockade-suppressed. Threats are stated intent; the attacks are verified. Physical action is the reality check.

United States

Said: CENTCOM asserts flows are "building momentum for freedom of navigation" and cites 1.25 billion barrels facilitated in recent months. Trump announced a Russian diesel supply deal and his administration floated easing shadow-fleet sanctions.

Ordered / done: The naval blockade of Iranian ports remains enforced (Ocean Molica disabled 10 Oct); convoy escorts continue. Reported consideration of very large conventional weapons for possible future strikes is contingency planning, not an order to attack, and no strike order has been verified.

Israel

Said: Restraint messaging continues through mediators; no new Israeli strike on Iran was verified in the primary window.

Actually done: No confirmed new strike inside the 15–16 October window. Political rhetoric may soften before physical energy risk does.

Ansar Allah (the Houthis)

Claimed: Strikes on Aramco facilities in Dhahran, King Khalid International Airport and a Riyadh refinery; airports and oil facilities declared legitimate targets; airlines warned off Saudi airports.

Actually damaged: Aramco HQ hit 13 Oct with a multi-floor fire (Reuters-sourced); Riyadh airport attack 10 Oct killed 13. A missile was intercepted over Riyadh on 14 Oct. Houthi claims exceed verified damage; treat claims as claims.

Saudi Arabia

Claimed: The energy ministry described the Dhahran attack as causing a "limited fire"; the coalition reported intercepting a missile over Riyadh.

Production: No verified production loss. Reuters reports Saudi output remains below its pre-war peak but recovered from wartime lows, and Aramco operations continued normally after the HQ strike. Infrastructure damage is not the same thing as production loss.

Qatar / QatarEnergy

Said: Force majeure extended: Edison notified of six further cancelled cargoes, taking the total to 35 (~4.6 bcm) and pushing the force majeure window to early December.

Physically: September exports ~1.6 MT (~76% below pre-war); October currently tracking ~0.6 MT (~91% below). Ras Laffan repair costs estimated around $20bn a year in lost revenue with up to five years to repair. Qatar needs predictable safe passage, not occasional successful crossings.

Shipping Risk

Hormuz oil

  • Pre-war baseline: ~17.13m bpd (Kpler Total Hormuz Clearance)
  • Latest seven-day average to 13 Oct: ~11.62m bpd
  • Difference: -5.51m bpd, around 32% below the pre-war baseline

Kpler's clearance measure is designed to capture oil movement more accurately than visible AIS counts, which matters because dark transits now dominate. Vessel traffic through the strait overall remains roughly 75% below pre-conflict levels (JMIC/NCAGS comparison), a different measure from the oil-volume figure.

Qatar LNG

  • Pre-war: ~6.75 MT/month (~81 MT in 2025)
  • September: ~1.6 MT, around 76% below pre-war; Reuters described September as barely one-quarter of normal
  • October current track: ~0.6 MT, around 91% below pre-war if the trajectory holds

The war is hitting LNG far harder than oil. LNG depends on integrated production, liquefaction, specialised vessels and predictable shipping; around 17% of Qatar's export capacity was damaged and Ras Laffan repairs may take up to five years.

Gulf of Oman / Qatar / UAE approaches

The risk chain now runs Persian Gulf to Strait of Hormuz to Gulf of Oman to Fujairah, Oman, Qatar and UAE approaches, then open sea. The 11 October UAV strike on a tanker at Sohar anchorage and attacks on vessels at UAE anchorages confirm that clearing Hormuz may no longer mean clearing the danger zone. Ten supertankers bound for Fujairah reportedly changed course during the 11–13 October attacks (Bloomberg-reported).

Bab al-Mandeb / Red Sea / Yanbu

Houthi activity around Bab al-Mandeb continues alongside the Saudi escalation, and territorial control and actual shipping flow are different measures. The Red Sea and Yanbu route is carrying a growing share of Saudi crude as bypass volumes rise, which also raises the stakes of any Houthi move against Red Sea shipping.

Alternative routes

Saudi East-West pipeline to Yanbu, the UAE Habshan-Fujairah pipeline, Fujairah and Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship transfers, SUMED/Suez and Cape routing are all working. Kpler says roughly 40% of Middle East crude now bypasses Hormuz versus about 17% pre-war, and total non-Iran exports are back near pre-conflict levels. Hormuz flow loss does not equal the same-sized loss in total Middle East oil exports.

Cost of moving it

Marine fuel prices remain more than 70% above pre-war levels (Reuters), war-risk insurance and tanker rates are elevated, and owners withdrew LNG tonnage from the region during flare-ups. A route can remain open and still carry a material war-risk premium. Friction gets priced.

Visible ship count and physical energy volume are different measures.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

Wholesale has moved. Have supplier books caught up?

Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached approximately 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew books, suspended pricing and returned higher. That is internal Switch365 commercial evidence, not a formal market threshold, and the public NBP indication is already printing above 210p with the internal W-26 working reference at 210.29p.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. Friday's internal curve moved up between +1.00p and +4.25p on every gas season, which means quotes issued earlier in the week may already be below replacement pricing. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.

A valid quote in hand this morning is a perishable asset. Compare it against Friday replacement pricing before it expires.
