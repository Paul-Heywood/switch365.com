Section 05

Futures Market Explained

Why prices differ by delivery period, and what Tuesday's moves do and do not mean

How the Curve Works

UK gas trades at the National Balancing Point (NBP) and European gas at the Dutch TTF. Each contract is a delivery period: a month, quarter or season such as Summer 2027 (April to September 2027) or Winter 2027 (October 2027 to March 2028). Today's curve is in steep backwardation: near delivery is expensive, later delivery is cheaper. Tuesday's W-26 working gas reference is 208.89p, while Summer 2028 is indicated at 88p.

A supplier business tariff is not the wholesale price. It blends the wholesale cost across the delivery period with network charges, policy costs, supplier margin, credit risk and your meter profile, and it is only valid within the quote window the supplier sets.

Why Prices Can Fall While Risk Rises

Tuesday's lower seasonal offers can coexist with real maritime risk because markets price the net of many forces: recovering Norwegian supply expectations, softer gas-for-power demand, a returning global oil surplus, and actual supplier rate-book changes can outweigh a shipping risk premium on any given morning.

Different delivery-period prices are quotes for different products, not predictions of what it will cost to buy in 2028. The 1 Year Ago line shows how much the market has moved, but the latest line is the customer's decision point. And a crucial discipline: the last trade on a screen is not the official settlement, and neither is your supplier's rate.

"Backwardation does not mean wait." A cheaper 2028 price quoted today is an opportunity to review a future-start contract now. It is not a promise that 2028 prices will still be cheap if you buy later.

Procurement Window

A two-sided read of Tuesday's falling curve. No market bottom is declared; no universal fix length is suggested.

Risks That Could Push Supplier Prices Up

  • Further UKMTO or operator-confirmed tanker incidents, sustained war-risk insurance pressure, or reduced laden LNG transit through Hormuz, distinguished from extra unsupported claims.
  • A new operator-confirmed outage at Qatar's Ras Laffan, the Saudi East-West pipeline or Yanbu, as opposed to a strike claim or interception.
  • European storage at 70.39% (Germany 57.28%) on the 15 November gas day, with further weather-driven draws, weak wind, higher gas burn or stronger Asian LNG competition.
  • Norwegian restart delays at Troll or Visund, or lower measured Gassco exports than schedules imply.
  • Supplier withdrawals or repricing confirmed by named supplier data. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.

Conditions That Could Pull Prices Lower

  • Verified restoration of Norwegian pipeline flows, stronger US LNG deliveries, safer repeated LNG exits from Qatar, or credible mine-clearance and insurance improvements.
  • Milder weather, stronger wind output and reduced gas-fired generation moderating storage withdrawals.
  • A growing crude surplus weighing on broader energy sentiment. This affects oil directly and UK gas only indirectly, not by the same percentage.
  • New genuine supplier tariffs showing Tuesday's offered declines in executable quotes for eligible customers.

How to Use This Window

Verify the existing contract end date and future-start eligibility, then compare full forecast bills across valid options: supplier, product, term, unit rate, standing charge, commission disclosure, pass-throughs, credit position, quote validity and risk tolerance. "Waiting is not neutral, and it only works if prices move in the customer's favour." This is a review-now window, not a blind sign-everything-now instruction.

Section 17

Supplier Price-Book Lag

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.

All of Tuesday's user-supplied seasonal price indicators fell, as did both W-26 working comparators. That makes supplier repricing and quote validity the commercially important question, but it does not prove every supplier has cut every customer tariff, or that any older quote remains available. "Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger."

This report does not invent named supplier tariffs. What we can state honestly:

  • Public wholesale gas fell hard on Monday (ICE NBP Dec-26 down roughly 7% to a 211.11p settlement) and internal offered indicators fell again on Tuesday morning.
  • Such moves typically reach business rate books unevenly: some suppliers reprice daily, others hold rates for set windows, and withdrawn rates cannot be recovered retrospectively.
  • The BG 18-month renewal window remains relevant for eligible British Gas customers, subject to product and customer confirmation.

Action: treat any existing valid quote as perishable. Validate its timestamp, unit rate, standing charge, commission, pass-throughs and expiry against fresh Tuesday offers before assuming either is executable. "The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow."

Customer Action

Five situations, five evidence-based next steps. Review live pricing now; never blindly contract immediately.

Situation 1

Existing Valid Quote

Check whether it remains executable: validate the quote date and time, commission disclosure, customer-specific unit rate and standing charge, contract end date and consent. Compare it against fresh Tuesday offers where legally and operationally possible.

Situation 2

Contract Ends in 2026

Confirm the exact end date and that no overlapping supply obligation exists, then prevent out-of-contract exposure. Compare valid near-term offers promptly; even after recent wholesale falls, deemed rates remain the expensive default.

Situation 3

Inside 12 Months

"Do not wait for renewal panic." Get current future-start quotes across available products and terms, review full forecast costs and the customer's risk tolerance. Quote validity must be real, not a model.

Situation 4

British Gas Inside 18 Months

Check BG up-to-18-month renewal eligibility where applicable. The future-start contract begins when the existing contract expires; the customer does not pay the new tariff today. Confirm eligibility per product and customer.

Situation 5

2027 / 2028 / 2029 / 2030 CED

S-28 gas sits 57.9% below W-26 and S-28 power 54.0% below the modelled W-26: different delivery periods, not whole-bill savings. Verify term eligibility and full price; indicative values can change.

Both Outcomes

Waiting Is Not Neutral

Waiting might help if Norwegian and US LNG supply improves, weather turns mild, wind rises, Gulf trade gets safer or suppliers cut rates further. Waiting might hurt if shipping losses cut physical LNG, Norway suffers new outages, storage and winter demand tighten, or supplier books reverse. Waiting only works if the market moves in the customer's favour. Choose the risk trade-off consciously, with evidence-based quote comparison.
