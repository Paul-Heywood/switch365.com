THE MARKET IS PRICING IN PEACE, BUT GAS SUPPLY IS STILL VULNERABLE. IS THIS THE BUYING WINDOW?

Gas prices have fallen sharply as traders price a lower risk of escalation, but supplier forward prices have not moved uniformly.

The energy market has continued to unwind the geopolitical premium built up during October. Switch365's W-26 gas working reference has moved from 220.60p to 199.84p/therm across the user-designated Monday-to-Tuesday comparison, a 9.4% decline. Diplomacy and expectations of less military escalation have been important drivers. But the W-26 comparison is an internal working reference, not a matched exchange settlement, and today's longer-dated supplier prices tell a more complicated story.

There has been genuine, partial improvement in physical supply. New ship-tracking data show 31 loaded LNG tankers leaving Hormuz in October, up from 23 in September. However, Qatar's LNG loadings remain well below pre-war volumes, damaged production trains are not repaired, Europe's gas storage is around 72%, and Norwegian production still faces disruption. The prospect of more available LNG is easing sentiment, but the system is not yet back to normal.

For UK businesses, this is a potential buying window, not a guaranteed bottom. While near-term working references are lower, most of the seasonal gas prices in today's supplied comparison have risen. That divergence makes supplier selection, contract start dates and forward-year pricing more important than ever. Customers should review genuinely available whole-bill quotes now, weighing the risk of a fresh price shock against the possibility of further falls if diplomacy and cargo flows keep improving.

The opportunity is to use today's market correction intelligently, not to guess the lowest price or pressure every customer to fix.

5.FUTURES MARKET EXPLAINED

Why the curve matters more than the headline.

Wholesale gas and power are bought for future delivery in seasons: Summer (S) runs April to September, Winter (W) runs October to March. "W-26" is the winter now in delivery; S-27, W-27 and beyond are forward delivery periods that suppliers price into business contracts months or years ahead.

A supplier's fixed-price offer is built from these forward prices plus non-commodity costs, standing charges, credit risk, hedging and margin. That is why a fall in the near-term market does not automatically appear in every customer quote, and why wholesale markets move first while supplier books often follow. The curve currently sits in backwardation: later delivery periods are priced below the distressed near-term. Backwardation does not mean wait, and cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later: a lower S-28 offered quote today is not a forecast that 2028 will be cheap to buy when it arrives.

The W-26 figures in this report are Switch365 working references. Monday's gas value was modelled, Tuesday's was user-supplied, and power is estimated at 90% of the relative gas move. They are decision tools, not exchange settlements, and are always labelled as such.

7.PROCUREMENT WINDOW

If a lower geopolitical risk premium is outpacing the recovery in physical LNG supply, does today's supplier forward curve offer a sensible buying window? A buying window is not a prediction of the market bottom.

RISK

  • Qatar's two damaged Ras Laffan trains (about 12.8 mtpa nameplate) are not repaired; some force majeure obligations remain under pressure and repeat Hormuz transit security and insurance are uncertain.
  • GIE snapshot 9 Nov: EU 72.38%, Germany 58.95%, with winter withdrawals under way and potential spot-LNG competition. Price exposure, not proof GB faces physical gas exhaustion.
  • Visund shut since 4 Nov; Gassco provisional return 16 Nov, subject to operator confirmation.
  • Diplomacy could fail, weather could turn colder, and another verified physical interruption could restore risk premium quickly.

BEARISH BUFFERS

  • 31 laden LNG vessels transited Hormuz outbound in October versus 23 in September (Reuters/S&P, published 10 Nov); Kpler counted 42 October loadings versus 27 in September. Partial recovery, not normality: Ras Laffan ~2.7m tonnes in October versus ~6.2m tonnes in February.
  • US and other non-Gulf LNG remains strong: 6.89m tonnes of US LNG to Europe in October (Reuters/LSEG, 2 Nov).
  • US–Iran diplomatic optimism and Trump's reduced-strike rhetoric have cut the risk premium; Brent around $91.57-91.88 early Tuesday (WSJ/Reuters). Oil is not NBP.
  • TTF December settled €82.33/MWh Monday, down 3.81% on Friday. A credible deal with regular safe transits could push wholesale lower still.

CUSTOMER ACTION

  • Potential buying window means review and compare, not automatic lock-in. In the user-designated comparison 6 of 8 gas forward periods rose even as the W-26 proxy fell 9.41%. Verify real supplier books before describing any forward period as cheaper.
  • Compare eligible future-start prices with the same period and same supplier on earlier quotations: rates, standing charges, pass-through costs, commission disclosure, credit, meter, CED, validity and term.
  • Evaluate 1, 2 and 3-year options and the British Gas up to 18-month renewal window where genuinely applicable. Explain both the risks of fixing and the risks of waiting.
  • No invented shortage, no claim that prices cannot fall further, no false closing time.
Conclusion: a sharp near-term correction can create opportunities, but you must see the actual forward price and customer-specific quote, because supplier book lag means not every period is now cheaper. This is a review-now window, not a blind sign-everything-now instruction.

8.BRITISH GAS 18-MONTH WINDOW

The extended renewal window turns market intelligence into a practical customer action.

British Gas allows eligible business customers to agree a renewal up to 18 months before their current contract end date, with the new agreement starting when the existing one ends. That window is unusually wide for the market and matters precisely now: customers do not need to catch the exact bottom to remove risk, they need a genuinely available price they can judge against their own history and today's curve.

With the W-26 working reference down 9.41% but most seasonal gas offers up in the latest comparison, a British Gas customer inside the window should request a live quote now and compare it against both their current rates and the seasonal indicators in Sections 3 and 4. Eligibility is not universal and must be confirmed per customer; the new contract always starts at the original contract end date. 14.CEASEFIRE REALITY CHECK

10 November primary test: has the US / Qatar / Iran dialogue produced a signed, operative deal, independently reliable regular Hormuz transit and rising verified shipped volumes, or have markets merely priced a better probability of those outcomes?

Answer today: probability, not paper. Monday's White House meeting and Trump's "militarily, effectively over" remark moved prices, and Iran's two-day implementation offer is a genuine negotiating signal. But Rubio called Tehran's demands "unacceptable" as recently as Sunday, the naval blockade and sanctions remain, and no deal text exists. An election result, a planned meeting or a diplomatic statement cannot be called a ceasefire. The new S&P/Kpler figures establish a partial October cargo-flow recovery (31 laden outbound tankers, 42 loadings), not normalisation.

  • Were the reported missiles and drones intercepted without meaningful energy damage, and did Ras Tanura have any verified refinery or export consequence? Independent site confirmations remain absent.
  • Are Saudi Eastern Region fields, Ras Tanura, Abqaiq, Dhahran, the East-West pipeline, Yanbu and Rabigh operating with actual output and export evidence? No verified loss.
  • Has Egypt moved from legal authority (26 Oct) to a formal Saudi request and verified deployment? Not established.
  • Have the October talks and the conditional Hormuz proposal become a signed, enforceable ceasefire with safe maritime transit? No.
  • Do UKMTO and ship operators confirm repeatable safe LNG and oil passage through Hormuz, the Gulf of Aden and Bab al-Mandeb? Not yet; incidents continued into 1 November.
  • Are Qatar's loadings rising sustainably, or do US spot cargoes still compensate? October improved; February levels are distant.
  • Is falling Brent accompanied by lower TTF/NBP and real supplier books? TTF Dec-26 fell 3.81% Monday; supplier seasonals mostly rose. Relief is incomplete at the customer end.
Mandatory distinctions: diplomatic contact is not a ceasefire, and a ceasefire announcement is not verified safe passage. An intercepted missile is not a confirmed energy infrastructure hit, and a hit is not necessarily lost production. An improvement in security can reduce a risk premium before damaged infrastructure is repaired, but physical flows are the supply test.

17.SUPPLIER PRICE-BOOK LAG

WHOLESALE HAS MOVED. HAVE SUPPLIER BOOKS CAUGHT UP?

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. The commercial question on Tuesday 10 November is why near-term W-26 working references are falling while six of eight seasonally offered gas indicators rose in the latest user-designated comparison. Monday's W-26 was modelled and Tuesday's gas was user-supplied, so the −9.41% working change is not independently demonstrated as a retail discount, and a move in an index or working comparator does not mean the customer's full bill changed by the same percentage.

Price-book opportunity is two-sided. A supplier may still be offering yesterday's higher rate, or it may have repriced lower overnight. Some offers may disappear; others may improve. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.

The test is executable quotes: same supplier, same start date, same term, with standing charges, pass-through components, credit, CED, meter and validity confirmed. Do not invent a "lock before 10 am" deadline; do verify how long each quote is genuinely valid.
