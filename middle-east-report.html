How to read the curve

Futures Market Explained

Different delivery periods answer different customer questions.

Front / near term

Immediate scarcity and risk premium

W-26 is most exposed to current LNG availability, weather, storage, shipping and geopolitical risk. Its 6.1% move does not mean every later season must rise by the same amount.

2027 and 2028

Persistent risk, not permanent parity

Higher S-27, W-27, S-28 and W-28 gas indicators show that risk is not confined to the nearest period. Yet their large discount to W-26 shows the curve still expects later conditions to differ.

2029 and 2030

Additional supply and demand assumptions

Longer maturities incorporate expected LNG projects, infrastructure, demand and policy. S-29 and S-30 gas softened, while W-29 and W-30 firmed. That mixed shape deserves attention, not a simplified headline.

Backwardation does not mean wait. It describes relative prices, not the direction of your next available quote.

What the offered range can tell us

  • How internal indicative levels changed between two snapshots
  • Which delivery periods currently sit below the near-term reference
  • Where a customer should request a firm, executable comparison
  • Whether the curve moved consistently or by delivery period

What it cannot tell us alone

  • A guaranteed supplier rate for a specific business
  • A supplier’s book status without a live check
  • The final bill, standing charge or non-commodity cost
  • A reliable forecast from a backwardated curve alone

Decision window

Procurement Window

The task is to review executable options, not to predict the exact market bottom.

Now to 90 days

Protect against exposure and book changes

Near-term internal references rose sharply. Check current contract status, renewal notices and live rates immediately. Waiting can expose the business to a changed book or default arrangement.

Within 12 months

Use the available runway

Request future-start prices against the actual contract end date. Compare like-for-like term, volume, payment and credit terms. Do not wait for renewal panic.

2027 to 2030 delivery

Price the correct season

Use the curve to identify where to investigate, then obtain executable offers. S-28 and W-28 deserve attention because both fuels sit roughly 50% to 56% below their W-26 working levels.

Before requesting quotes

  • Confirm contract end date and termination notice deadline
  • Validate annual consumption and meter details
  • Separate gas and power delivery periods
  • Check credit, site changes and expected demand
  • Ask for quote validity and any book restrictions

Before accepting an offer

  • Compare total contract value, not unit rate alone
  • Check standing charges and non-commodity treatment
  • Confirm start date, duration and volume tolerance
  • Read pass-through, change-in-law and termination clauses
  • Record the quote timestamp and validity
Waiting is not neutral. Market levels, supplier risk appetite, credit terms and quote availability can all change while a customer waits.

Ceasefire Reality Check

Active contacts are not the same as a verified de-escalation mechanism.

Confirmed

Defensive military support

The UK confirmed RAF Typhoon defensive counter-air missions and no offensive strikes against the Houthis. This may reduce attack success risk; it does not establish a ceasefire.

Developing

Regional support remains incomplete

Turkey remained at parliamentary-mandate stage for possible defensive deployment. France’s Yanbu support was an earlier commitment with incomplete public operational detail.

Not verified

No comprehensive settlement found

Diplomatic contacts continued, but this run found no verified comprehensive ceasefire, safe-passage agreement or restoration of impaired LNG capacity.

Diplomacy can cap the tail risk. It cannot yet be priced as a completed physical repair.

Timing advantage and execution risk

Supplier Price-Book Lag

Internal offered indicators can move before every supplier book has visibly repriced.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale moved first. Supplier books may still follow.
Opportunity

A live book may not fully reflect the latest move

If a supplier is still quoting from an earlier risk position, an eligible customer may be able to secure value before a reprice. Only a timestamped executable quote can establish this.

Danger

Availability can change without notice

Risk committees can widen margins, restrict terms, change credit requirements or withdraw a book. No fresh withdrawal was publicly verified in this run, so none is claimed.

Action

Request, timestamp and compare

Ask for live offers now. Record quote validity, start date and terms. Compare against the correct season and total contract value rather than an internal indicator alone.

Do not describe an internal range as an offer that is guaranteed to exist. It is evidence for a procurement conversation. The supplier’s executable quote is the decision document.
