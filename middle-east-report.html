Summary

QatarEnergy, one of the world's biggest LNG exporters, is buying American spot LNG to meet customer commitments while locking US gas into its portfolio for decades. Iranian strikes on 18 to 19 March damaged two of Qatar's 14 LNG trains at Ras Laffan, removing approximately 12.8 mtpa of liquefaction capacity, roughly 17% of export capacity. The original three to five year repair outlook was tightened in September to around three years for the two trains. Actual export flows remain a fraction of pre-war levels: September loadings of roughly 1.4 to 1.6 million tonnes were a seven-month high but still about three-quarters below the pre-war monthly average, and October is tracking lower again after renewed attacks on shipping. Verified physical fact

On 22 October, QatarEnergy signed sale and purchase agreements with Cheniere for up to 4 mtpa, 2 mtpa each from Sabine Pass and Corpus Christi, free on board at Henry Hub plus a fixed fee, with deliveries beginning in the early 2030s and continuing through 2054. It is separately in advanced talks with Venture Global for at least 3 mtpa, but that contract is unsigned and its start date and duration are undisclosed. Neither arrangement is current replacement supply: Cheniere delivers nothing before 2031, and the proposed Venture Global volume equals only 23.4% of the damaged annual capacity. The market issue is that a major exporter is simultaneously supplying less of its own gas and competing for the same flexible Atlantic Basin cargoes that Europe and Asia want. Operator confirmation Analysis

Overnight, the Saudi security picture deteriorated but the physical supply position did not. A drone targeting Shaybah's processing facility was intercepted on 21 October, killing two workers; the Saudi Energy Ministry says operations were unaffected. Dammam's airport was attacked the same evening, and a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile off Bukha near the Hormuz approaches. Iran's IRGC claims seven vessels were hit, which remains an unverified claim. UK wholesale gas is trading near its highest levels since 2022, and Switch365's internal offered range moved up across near-term 2027 contracts this morning, with Friday's indicative W-26 working reference at 222.32p/therm for gas and £170.49/MWh for power. Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger: the market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow. Switch365 internal data

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their existing contract ends to see today's future-delivery price. When suppliers offer a Summer 2028 or Winter 2029 rate today, that is a live, executable view of the forward curve, subject to eligibility and book availability.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. It means future delivery is cheaper today.
Backwardation does not mean wait. Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.

Friday's internal range is backwardated: near-term winter risk is priced high because the Qatar LNG shortfall, Hormuz shipping risk and low storage are problems for the next two winters, while the market expects new supply, led by US LNG expansion, to ease the balance later in the decade. That is a statement about today's curve, not a promise that 2029 prices will fall. The % vs W-26 column shows the current forward-value gap.

British Gas 18-Month Window

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing. Switch365 internal evidence

The contract does not start today. It begins when the existing agreement ends. The 18-month window turns today's forward curve into something customers may actually be able to secure: a British Gas customer whose contract ends in, for example, Spring 2028 can now test live pricing against a market where S-28 gas is offered at 101.50p/therm and W-28 at 106.00p, roughly 52% to 54% below the indicative W-26 reference.

British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action. Eligibility is not automatic for every customer, and once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.

Supplier Price-Book Lag: Wholesale Has Moved. Have Supplier Books Caught Up?

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. This morning's internal range shows the following has started: 2027 gas offers rose 4.25p to 4.50p overnight, and S-27 power rose £2.00. But books reprice unevenly, by supplier, by season and by time of day, which is precisely why valid quotes from earlier this week can still sit below the new market.

Switch365 internal commercial experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, some supplier books were withdrawn, suspended or returned higher. That is internal experience, not an official market trigger, but the indicative W-26 reference is already at 222.32p.

Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.
The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.
