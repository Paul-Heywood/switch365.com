1Summary

Report date: 15 October 2026 · Primary research window: 14 to 15 October · Extended verification window: 9 to 15 October (Europe/London)

Two commercial vessels have sunk near the Strait of Hormuz within roughly 24 hours, taking confirmed ship losses since the US-Iran war began on 28 February to three. The Singapore-flagged container ship GFS Pride sank after being struck and catching fire, with all 21 crew evacuated, while the dry-bulk carrier Cape Dao, originally hit on 23 September, has also now sunk. Reuters describes them as the second and third ship losses of the war, and they arrive during the sharpest sustained rise in attacks on commercial shipping since the conflict began.

Iran is widening its maritime campaign beyond the Strait of Hormuz into the Gulf of Oman and towards waters near Qatar and the UAE. That matters for UK energy because Qatar LNG, already severely impaired, is the number-one direct UK gas exposure: September exports recovered only to about 1.6 million tonnes, less than a quarter of pre-war normal, October is tracking near 0.6 million tonnes, and Edison says QatarEnergy has indicated its contracted Qatari deliveries will not resume until at least December.

The political and physical pictures are diverging. Trump and Netanyahu have both signalled restraint over fresh direct strikes on Iran, yet ships are still being struck and lost, and Brent is falling towards $104 because Middle East crude exports have recovered to around 80% of pre-war levels while LNG has not. TTF and NBP eased at the front of the curve on 15 October morning pricing, but the Switch365 offered ranges remain in steep backwardation, and the commercial question is unchanged: what can a customer actually secure today, before supplier books move again?

Overnight in 30 seconds

  • Aramco operations continue despite substantial damage to its Dhahran headquarters in the 13 October Houthi attack; a further missile towards Riyadh was intercepted on 14 October.
  • Brent is falling despite the security deterioration because crude supply remains relatively resilient; TTF also eased around 1.6% on the morning of 15 October.

What it means for UK energy

Shipping losses raise the probability of further LNG restriction, and LNG is the bridge between Gulf security and UK gas; but with Norway flowing strongly, US LNG at record levels and storage at 74%, the market currently has buffers, which is why front-end prices can soften even as physical risk rises. Buffers are not immunity: supplier books can still reprice quickly, and the deep backwardation in the Switch365 ranges means several future delivery periods can currently be secured far below near-term winter pricing where supplier windows allow.

Supplier books. With S-27 and W-27 power ticking up while the front eased, books are re-slicing risk across seasons rather than simply following the headline. Customers with 2027 starts should not assume today's mixed curve will still be available next week.

5Futures Market Explained

Concise working principles for reading today's offered ranges.

  • The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period.
  • A customer does not have to wait until their current contract ends to see today's future-delivery price.
  • Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.
  • Backwardation does not mean wait. It means that future delivery period is cheaper today.
  • The question is whether the customer can secure that period now.
  • Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.
Today's curve is in steep backwardation precisely because near-term winter risk (Qatar LNG, Hormuz, storage) is priced into W-26 and W-27, while later periods trade far lower. The opportunity is the gap itself, and it only exists while supplier windows allow it to be locked.

8British Gas 18-Month Window

Verified Switch365 internal evidence.

  • British Gas has extended its business-energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months.
  • This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing.
  • The new agreement does not start today. It starts when the current contract ends.
  • The 18-month window matters because it can make today's forward-value gap actionable.
  • Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.

In practical terms: a customer whose contract end date falls within 18 months may now be able to review pricing for S-27, W-27 or beyond, exactly the periods sitting 23% to 55% below W-26 on today's sheet. British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action. Eligibility is not automatic for every customer, so the first step is to check eligibility and live pricing against the actual contract end date.

10Top Five Warning Signs

The five developments that would most directly change what a UK business can secure.

1. An LNG carrier is sunk or seriously disabled

Direct global-gas escalation. Twelve attacks on liquefied-gas carriers have already been recorded this month; a loss would move TTF and NBP within hours.

2. Ship losses continue to increase

Three wartime losses becomes a persistent pattern. Two sinkings within roughly 24 hours is already a step-change from damage-only incidents.

3. Qatar LNG falls further or delivery delays extend

Direct European gas consequence. October tracking near 0.6Mt and Edison's December delay are the markers to beat, in either direction.

4. Saudi energy infrastructure suffers verified production loss

Physical oil impact replaces infrastructure-risk headline. Dhahran HQ damage did not meet this test; a hit on Abqaiq, Ras Tanura or East-West throughput would.

5. TTF / NBP rise and suppliers pull books

Direct customer consequence. Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached approximately 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew books, suspended pricing and returned higher.

15Shipping Risk

Route-by-route. Three commercial vessels have now been lost since the war began.

Strait of Hormuz: SEVERE

  • GFS Pride (container) sank after strike and fire; all 21 crew evacuated. Iran's IRGC claimed a mine; Reuters reported a projectile strike
  • Cape Dao (bulk carrier) sank following damage from the 23 September attack; one seafarer was killed in that strike
  • UKMTO 14 October entries: a 19,000-tonne container ship struck outbound and a 50,000-tonne container ship struck inbound; both initially reported minor damage and continued
  • IMO count: at least 24 seafarers killed in 106 confirmed incidents since the war began
  • Traffic about 75% below pre-conflict levels; roughly 29 US-facilitated transits per day against a historical average near 138
  • Substantial oil still moves under escort and dark transits; LNG remains far weaker

Gulf of Oman: HIGHER RISK

  • US CENTCOM disabled the Ocean Molica on 10-11 October after it attempted to breach the blockade; propulsion disabled, crew unharmed
  • UKMTO reported a military encounter involving a tanker in the Gulf of Oman the same day

17Supplier Price-Book Lag

WHOLESALE HAS MOVED. HAVE SUPPLIER BOOKS CAUGHT UP?

Overnight the Switch365 sheet moved in both directions at once: front-end gas and power eased, while mid-curve gas (W-28 through S-30) and 2027 power firmed. That is exactly the environment where supplier price books lag, and where lag cuts both ways for customers.

  • Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger.
  • Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.
  • Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.
  • The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.

The chain runs: wholesale moves; the supplier book may temporarily lag; an existing quote may remain valid; the supplier hedge catches up; the rate is withdrawn or repriced; the customer loses the earlier price. Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached approximately 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew books, suspended pricing and returned higher. W-26 offered gas at 208.29p sits above that historical stress point, which is why book behaviour can change without warning even on a softer morning.

A valid quote that predates the latest wholesale move is a live asset. Compare it against today's replacement price before it expires.
