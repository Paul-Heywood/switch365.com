Yanbu Loadings Suspended as Saudi European Cargoes Are Cancelled

East-West Pipeline remains offline, Saudi Arabia turns to Sohar STS transfers, and Hormuz tracked traffic stays at four with no visible LNG tankers.

The freshest physical energy development. Reuters reported that crude loadings at Yanbu had been suspended and that Aramco had told some European buyers their late-September cargoes were cancelled. The East-West Pipeline remained offline at the last physical check. The duration of the loading suspension and the total affected volume remain unclear, and Aramco has not publicly confirmed the position. This is the first clear commercial cargo consequence of the pipeline outage.

Workarounds show resilience and disruption at the same time. Saudi Arabia is offering additional Arab Light, Arab Medium and Arab Heavy to Asian buyers via ship-to-ship transfers off Sohar, Oman, outside Hormuz, with loadings at Ras Tanura and Juaymah increasing. Kpler tracked four commodity-vessel transits through Hormuz on Tuesday, down from seven Monday, far below the recent ten-day average of around 18. Two entered laden, two exited. No VLCC or LNG tanker appeared among those four visible transits, and a VLGC carrying LPG exited via an Iranian route. AIS-dark movements are excluded.

Oil has reacted, gas has not copied it. Bab al-Mandeb tracked traffic eased to 22 vessels Tuesday from 24 Monday; the Strait remains physically active. A fresh Saudi-led coalition interception of a Houthi drone south of Mecca has produced no independently verified energy damage. Brent held above $108 at the Wednesday morning reference after Tuesday's 2.9% rally, and some prompt physical European cargoes traded above $130. NBP and TTF remain extremely elevated, but the latest comparable contracts did not produce an equivalent new surge. Supplier books, not headlines, set the price a customer can secure today.

Section 05

Futures Market Explained

The Core Principle

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their contract end date to see today's price for future delivery. The quotes on this page are offers for future delivery, available to review now.

Backwardation Is Not a Signal to Wait

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. Backwardation does not mean wait. A negative % vs W-26 means that future delivery period is currently cheaper than the front of the curve. It means there may be an opportunity now, while that period is still priced as future delivery. The customer mistake is thinking a cheaper future period means they should wait. It does not. It means we should check whether that future period can be secured now.

Timing Is the Value

The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period. Waiting gives the market time to remove today's forward-value opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.

How to Read the Lines

The 1 Year Ago line is context. The latest line is the customer's decision point. The 1 Year Ago line shows whether today's forward curve is cheaper, similar or more expensive than the same delivery period was priced last year. Use the 1 Year Ago line as context, not as the customer's available price. Today's latest price is the decision point. The 1 Year Ago line shows how the market has changed.

This is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market.

British Gas 18-Month Window

What Changed

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier chance to review future-start pricing. The 18-month British Gas window matters because it turns the forward market into a practical customer action.

Your new contract would not start today. It would begin when your current agreement ends. Waiting is not neutral. If the market moves or British Gas withdraws rates, customers cannot go back and secure today's pricing later. Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. It means check whether the future period can be secured now.

Why It Matters Today

  • Backwardation means the value sits in future delivery; the 18-month window makes those periods reachable earlier.
  • Supplier volatility is high: books can be withdrawn or repriced without notice.
  • Near-term markets remain extremely elevated; fixing the future-start period caps exposure to W-26 pricing.
  • Duration risk cuts both ways: prolonged disruption supports current forward-value, but a pipeline restart could reprice the curve.
  • Eligibility must be checked now; the window only has value while it is open.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.
Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.

Waiting for certainty can mean missing the price movement. Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale previously neared 180p/therm, some supplier books were withdrawn, suspended or reissued higher. Internal commercial evidence, not an official industry threshold.
