Futures Market Explained Why the delivery period matters more than today's headline price

What the futures market is

The futures market shows the price for energy delivered in future periods. A customer renewing from 2027, 2028, 2029 or 2030 is not only interested in today's price. They should look at what the market is currently offering for their actual delivery period.

Right now the front of the curve (W-26) carries the conflict premium from the Iran war, Hormuz disruption and the Red Sea blockade, while later periods are priced as if some normalisation eventually arrives. That shape — expensive front, cheaper back — is called backwardation.

What it means for a customer

  • Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.
  • The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period.
  • Waiting lets the market make the decision for the customer.
  • As time passes, that future period becomes the near-term market and can absorb the same risk premium.

The practical point: if a customer's contract end date sits in 2027, 2028 or beyond, the relevant question is not "where is the market today?" but "what can I secure today for the period my contract actually covers?" — and whether their supplier window lets them trade that period now.

Supplier Price-Book Lag The opportunity and the danger

  • Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger.
  • Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.
  • Waiting for certainty can mean missing the price movement.
  • Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and buy yesterday's price.
  • Even though oil has fallen from the highs, supplier books may still react to the move that has already happened.
  • Suppliers do not need a full closure of Hormuz or Bab el-Mandeb before they reprice.
  • Suppliers also do not need diplomacy to fail completely before they price renewed conflict risk.
  • When conflict spreads geographically — Jordan, Iraq, Iran, Egypt in 36 hours — suppliers can price the increased probability of disruption before physical closure happens.

Wholesale moved first — W-26 gas +5.50 p/therm and W-26 power +£3.50/MWh between Tuesday and Wednesday AM snapshots. Supplier books may still follow. That is the window.
