05Futures Market Explained

Why "futures" prices matter to a business energy bill

Suppliers buy the gas and electricity for your contract in advance, in seasonal blocks — Winter 2026 (W-26), Summer 2027 (S-27) and so on. The prices in Sections 03 and 04 are the offered levels for those blocks. When you fix a contract starting in October 2027, the supplier's cost is anchored to the W-27 season, not to today's spot price.

Backwardation is when nearer delivery periods cost more than later ones. Your supplied curve is strongly backwardated: W-26 gas at 142.75 p/th versus S-30 at 63.08 p/th. The market is charging a heavy premium for energy delivered through this coming winter — the conflict premium — and progressively less for each later year.

The two ways this resolves: either the front falls towards the back (peace, restored shipping, full storage), or the back rises towards the front (renewed escalation, route closures, cold winter on thin storage). Today's cheaper 2027–2030 seasons are not a promise that energy will be cheap then — they are a price you can secure now. If escalation returns, those seasons reprice upwards before the front does, because that is where the open risk sits.

14Ceasefire Reality Check

What is actually in place

  • A pause on direct US–Iran strikes, in place since late July and described in reporting as fragile and conditional — not a ceasefire treaty.[S10][S29]
  • An interim framework under strain: the US previously revoked the temporary Iranian-oil sanctions waiver after tanker attacks, and talks have already been suspended and resumed more than once.[S11]
  • Talks expected to resume today (03/08) with the White House holding off further strikes to give the track a chance.[S10][S12]

What is not in place

  • No final deal. Nothing signed, no verified text, no implementation timeline. Any claim of a done deal is unverified at publication.[S12]
  • No proxy pause. The strike pause covers US–Iran direct exchanges. It did not stop US–Saudi strikes in Iraq, Houthi threats, or weekend tanker incidents.[S20][S26]
  • No normalisation of Hormuz. Traffic is moving but reduced and under active UKMTO warnings; Iran's approved-route regime still stands.[S15]
Reality check: the market is trading a pause as if it were a settlement. A pause removes today's escalation; it does not remove the route risk, the proxy risk, or the 06/08 deadline. Relief pricing is reversible in a single session.

17Supplier Price-Book Lag

Suppliers do not reprice business contracts tick-by-tick. They update price books periodically and can withdraw them entirely when volatility spikes. That lag cuts both ways — and this year has already shown both edges:

  • Downside of the lag (March evidence): when the conflict escalated in late February/March, UK suppliers pulled or repriced fixed tariffs at speed — 57 tariffs removed or repriced in days in one analysis, with the FT and Utility Week documenting withdrawals across the market. Customers waiting for "a better day" lost whole products, not just price points.[S37][S38][S39]
  • Upside of the lag (May evidence): as volatility cooled into May, suppliers returned to quoting — but pricing stayed cautious and "can change quickly". Books reopened behind the market, giving well-timed buyers a brief window of stale, cheaper pricing.[S40]
  • Why it matters: wholesale is roughly 40% of a business electricity bill and about 60% of a gas bill (Ofgem). The lag between the traded curve and the printed book is where brokers add value — buying the curve before the book catches up.[S41]

The opportunity

Diplomacy headlines have softened traded prices this week. Price books revised after a relief move — but before any talks failure — can briefly offer 2027/2028 seasons cheaper than the live curve justifies. Quoting now, with same-day acceptance readiness, is how that window gets captured.

The danger

The same lag means quotes can be withdrawn or repriced intraday when headlines turn — as March proved. A quote in hand this week has a shelf life measured in days, not weeks. Treat every competitive offer against the 2027–2029 seasons as perishable.
