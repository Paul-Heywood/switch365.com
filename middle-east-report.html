Summary

Donald Trump has publicly ruled out a US attack on Iran before the 3 November midterm elections, writing on Truth Social that discussions with Tehran are "productive" and that the US "will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections". Reuters independently confirmed the statement. Tehran says it is reviewing Washington's response to its proposal under which the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within seven days, and expects to reply within days. Markets responded: Brent settled more than 4% higher on Thursday at $104.28/bbl on tanker attacks, hurricane disruption and strike fears, then fell around 1.3% to roughly $102.91/bbl early Friday on the no-strike signal.

A material short-term escalation risk has been removed from the market for now. It is not a peace agreement. The US naval blockade of Iranian ports remains in force, and the US imposed fresh sanctions on Thursday targeting 17 vessels and additional Iranian networks under "Operation Economic Outcast". The US Treasury says Iran has stopped loading and unloading crude vessels. Iran's Revolutionary Guards warned they are ready to respond to "unauthorised passage" in Hormuz, and Reuters reports Iran has warned regional states it has plans to block export routes it has not approved. Hormuz is not open as usual: Kpler tracked commodity transits falling to seven on Tuesday, the lowest in more than two months, edging up to ten on Wednesday, with crude flow of at least 10.1 million barrels a day.

For UK energy the decisive chain remains Hormuz, Qatar LNG, TTF, NBP and then supplier books. Qatar-linked LNG transits improved through late September and early October, but remain a fraction of pre-war levels and depend on safe passage. Norway is carrying technical outages (Dvalin and Heidrun curtailed roughly 14.7 mcm/d on 7 October), European storage is around 73% and roughly 15 points below the seasonal norm, and Hurricane Isaias has shut in about 62.9% of US Gulf oil and 57% of US Gulf gas production. Switch365 internal offered pricing shows the front of the gas curve easing overnight (W-26 down 4.92p to 193.21p/therm) while 2027 periods firmed. The commercial position: this is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market.

What it means for UK energy

The diplomatic signal is genuinely bearish for the immediate war premium, and the front of the UK gas curve has started to reflect it. But nothing physical has normalised: the blockade continues, Hormuz passage is still attacked and intimidated, Qatar LNG is still routing through a militarised strait, storage is low for the season, and a hurricane is removing US supply. Supplier books lag wholesale in both directions. Customers with live decisions in 2026 to 2030 delivery periods should review today's offered range now rather than assume the de-escalation will be passed on, or that it will last.

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their contract end date to see today's price for future delivery.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. A summer 2028 price below today's winter 2026 price is not a promise that summer 2028 will be cheap when it arrives. It is the price available to secure today for that delivery period.

Backwardation does not mean wait. The customer mistake is assuming a cheaper future period will still be cheap when they eventually reach it. Waiting gives the market time to remove today's forward-value opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.

The % vs W-26 column shows the current forward-value gap. It compares each future period's latest offered price with the latest W-26 reference. A negative number means that period is cheaper than the front winter today.

The 1 Year Ago line shows how much the market has moved, but the latest line is the customer's decision point. It is context, not today's available price.

British Gas 18-Month Window

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing. (Verified Switch365 internal evidence.)

The contract does not start today. It begins when the existing agreement ends. The 18-month window turns today's forward curve into something customers may actually be able to secure.

Why it matters this morning: with S-27 gas at 149.00p (22.9% below W-26) and S-27 power at £117.00 (21.7% below W-26), the extended window lets eligible customers test those forward prices against their actual end date rather than waiting for the standard renewal cycle.

Once a rate is withdrawn, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. Not every customer is automatically eligible; eligibility and live pricing should be checked directly.

British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action.

Top Five Warning Signs

1. US-Iran talks break down

Iran's reply to the US response is due within days. A rejection, or fresh ultimatum language, would reverse the week's strongest bearish signal and likely reprice the front of the gas curve back up. Watch Tasnim / IRNA statements and Witkoff's channel.

2. US force posture contradicts the no-attack message

Reports (Israel's Channel 12) said US forces were told to prepare options for possible large-scale strikes within three weeks; Trump has previously used calm language before military action. If carriers, tanker aircraft or strike assets keep surging after the no-attack pledge, flag the contradiction. A feint is a scenario, not the base case, but posture is the test.

3. Qatar LNG transits fall

LNG transits improved through September but the recovery is fragile and October's pace is the test. Any fall in visible cargoes, fresh dark-transit surges or a QatarEnergy force majeure would have a direct UK gas consequence via TTF and NBP.

4. Hurricane Isaias disrupts US LNG terminals

Offshore gas shut-ins are not automatically LNG export shut-ins, but the Florida-Alabama landfall zone sits near Gulf Coast energy infrastructure. Feedgas declines, port closures or damage at Freeport, Cameron, Sabine Pass, Plaquemines or Corpus Christi would weaken Europe's main replacement supply. MMA's next update is due Friday afternoon.

5. NBP / TTF rise and suppliers pull books

Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm some suppliers pulled books, suspended pricing and returned higher. W-26 remains above that level at 193.21p. If the curve firms again, valid quotes can disappear the same day.

Shipping Risk

Hormuz

Hormuz is not open as usual. It is also not completely closed. Substantial traffic continues under attack and intimidation, some traffic has fallen, alternative routes are expanding, and large oil volumes still move. Kpler tracked commodity transits falling to seven on Tuesday (a two-month low, edging up to ten Wednesday) and crude flow of at least 10.1m bpd, about 74% of pre-war levels. Pre-war the strait handled roughly 125 large commercial vessels a day carrying about 20% of global oil and LNG supply.

Data-conflict note: Trump claims 22 million barrels crossed in one night; CENTCOM cited around 20 million barrels of crude moving; Kpler tracked 10.1m bpd of crude. Source, time period, product and measurement method differ. Visible vessel count and physical energy volume are different measures. Never treat one source as automatically cancelling another.

Latest incident: the Antigua and Barbuda-flagged chemical tanker Acers was struck by multiple projectiles about 51 nautical miles off Qatar in the past 24 hours, with minor crew injuries (UKMTO). Clearwater Dynamics warns the combat area may be widening towards Qatar, Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq approaches.

Iranian route threat

Reuters-sourced officials say Iran has warned regional states that opening new, unauthorised oil export routes would be treated as hostile, with plans to block them using missile and drone attacks on ships or boat intimidation. Gulf producers have been routing cargoes along the Omani side and transferring via ship-to-ship in the Gulf of Oman under US aerial cover; Iran's threat targets exactly those workarounds. A route can remain physically open and still carry a material war-risk premium. Friction gets priced.

Qatar LNG

September LNG transits were the highest since the war began (19 cargoes per S&P Global, 21 per Kpler, including dark transits), and four more Ras Laffan cargoes reappeared outside the strait around 2-3 October. S&P Global suggests October could reach about 25% of pre-war levels if the pace holds. Pre-war, roughly three LNG cargoes a day transited. LNG remains the bridge between Hormuz and UK gas. Pearl GTL's partial restart is infrastructure recovery, not LNG normalisation; North Field East's first train (start-up expected November 2026, LNG from Q1 2027) is medium-term supply, not today's cargo.

Bab al-Mandeb / Yemen

Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces launched a counter-offensive this week to recapture territory near the Bab al-Mandeb strait, with coalition air support. Territorial leverage and physical shipping are different measures: coastal gains do not restore safe passage while the Houthis retain ballistic missile and drone capability, as the Riyadh and Abha attacks demonstrate.

Red Sea / Saudi routes

Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline to Yanbu remains the key bypass for Hormuz-risked crude, and the western section of that pipeline is among the defensive priorities named for Makkah alliance support. Saudi exports via Gulf of Oman STS transfers have helped keep overall Middle East crude exports near pre-war levels even as strait transits fell. Any confirmed hit on Saudi energy infrastructure would change this picture immediately; none is confirmed.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

Wholesale has moved. Have supplier books caught up?

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. The chain runs: wholesale moves, the supplier book may remain unchanged temporarily, hedging cost catches up, the supplier reprices, the old quote disappears, the customer pays more.

This morning the chain is loaded in both directions. The front of the wholesale curve eased overnight on Trump's statement, so some books may briefly hold pricing that now looks rich against the new front. At the same time S-27 and W-27 firmed, so books referencing 2027 periods have upward pressure already baked in.

Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm some suppliers pulled books, suspended pricing and returned higher. W-26 sits at 193.21p, still above that level. This is internal experience, not an official market threshold.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.
