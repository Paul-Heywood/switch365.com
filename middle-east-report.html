QATAR LNG RECOVERY STALLS AS SAUDI-HOUTHI WAR ESCALATES

Middle Eastern crude exports have recovered strongly, but Qatar's LNG system remains heavily impaired. Qatar exported only around 1.6 million tonnes in September, less than a quarter of normal volumes, and October exports are currently tracking around just 0.6 million tonnes. At the same time, the Saudi-Houthi conflict has escalated into repeated attacks on airports, airspace and threatened energy infrastructure, while US-Iran negotiations over Hormuz remain stalled rather than broken.

01

Summary

The central energy story has changed. Oil has adapted to the war far better than LNG has, and for UK gas that difference matters more than the Brent headline.

Reuters and Kpler data show Qatar exported only around 1.6 million tonnes of LNG in September, less than one quarter of pre-war normal, with roughly a third of that staying inside the Gulf for Kuwait. October is currently tracking around just 0.6 million tonnes. QatarEnergy's force majeure has been extended, with reporting indicating deliveries to Asia and Europe are now affected through the end of November. Meanwhile Middle Eastern crude through Hormuz recovered to around 16 million barrels per day in September, roughly 80% of pre-war levels. Oil normalisation does not mean gas normalisation.

The Saudi-Houthi war has escalated sharply. Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport was struck four times in five days; Saturday's missile strike killed 12 people and wounded more than 300, the deadliest attack in a Gulf state since the Iran war began, and the airport is closed until at least 17 October. Dammam's airport was also attacked. Saudi Arabia reported a fresh barrage on Monday, with one missile towards Riyadh intercepted. Qatar Airways has suspended Riyadh and Dammam services, joining British Airways, KLM, Air India and Gulf Air in pulling Saudi flights.

US-Iran talks are stalled but alive: Tehran is still reviewing Washington's response after its security chief called it insufficient against Iran's seven Hormuz conditions. Europe enters winter with storage around 74% full, roughly 13 to 15 points below the five-year norm, while TTF front month sits near €84.5/MWh and public NBP indications are above 200p/therm. Switch365's offered W-26 gas reference moved up 6.80p to 200.01p/therm this morning. Wholesale has moved first; supplier books may still follow.

Overnight in 30 Seconds

  • Qatar Airways has suspended Riyadh and Dammam flights as Saudi aviation disruption spreads; Riyadh airport stays shut until at least 17 October.
  • Saudi Arabia intercepted a fresh missile towards Riyadh on Monday and resumed public missile and drone alerts after a pause that preceded the deadly weekend strikes.
  • Iran says it is still reviewing Washington's Hormuz response and will reply through intermediaries; its security chief called the US response insufficient.
  • Qatar LNG remains dramatically weaker than crude through Hormuz: September exports about 1.6MT versus crude flows around 80% of pre-war.
  • Europe is entering winter with storage only around 74% full; Germany near 60% and tracking about 77% EU-wide by 1 November, short of even the relaxed 80% target.
  • Brent is easing near $105 on Russian diesel supply prospects, but Middle East physical gas risk remains elevated. Brent is not the customer's gas quote.

What it means for UK energy

UK business gas and power remain priced off a tight global LNG pool, low European storage and an unresolved Hormuz standoff, not off the Brent headline. Offered forward seasons still sit 23% to 65% below winter-26 pricing: this is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market.

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period.

A customer does not have to wait until their contract ends to see today's price for future delivery. The offered table above is that price, live, by delivery season.

  • Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. S-28 gas at 91.00p is today's price for summer-28 delivery, not a forecast of what summer-28 will cost later.
  • Backwardation does not mean wait. It means the future delivery period is cheaper if the customer can access it now.
  • Waiting gives the market time to remove today's forward-value opportunity. If Qatar deteriorates, Hormuz worsens or winter bites, the far curve can reprice towards the front.
  • Waiting is not neutral. Every day a rate is not secured is a day the market can move it.

British Gas 18-Month Window

British Gas has extended its business-energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. (Switch365 internal evidence.)

  • This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing.
  • The new contract does not begin today. It starts when the existing agreement ends.
  • The 18-month window matters because it can turn the forward-value gap into an actionable customer opportunity: a customer whose contract end date falls inside 18 months can now test S-27 and W-27 pricing (151.00p / 143.25p gas; £118.30 / £118.10 power) against today's W-26 front (200.01p / £153.38).
  • Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.
British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action. Check eligibility and live pricing against the actual contract end date, not a diary assumption.

Top Five Warning Signs

1. QATAR OCTOBER LNG TRACK FALLS BELOW 0.6MT

October is already tracking around 0.6MT against September's 1.6MT. A further slide would signal physical deterioration beyond the current impairment and tighten the global LNG pool again before winter.

2. LNG CARRIERS STOP USING HORMUZ

21 cargoes exited in September, mostly dark. If even that trickle stops, the direct global-gas consequence lands on TTF and NBP within days.

3. SAUDI ENERGY INFRASTRUCTURE SUFFERS VERIFIED DAMAGE

The Houthis have claimed attacks on oil and gas facilities in the Eastern Province and threatened Aramco assets. Claim is not capacity loss, but Riyadh's interceptor shortage raises the probability of a successful strike. Verified damage converts threat into supply loss.

4. US-IRAN HORMUZ TALKS COLLAPSE

Talks are stalled but alive. A formal breakdown removes the diplomatic off-ramp and with it any near-term prospect of normalised LNG access through Hormuz.

5. NBP / TTF RISE AND SUPPLIERS PULL BOOKS

Offered W-26 gas crossed 200p this morning. Switch365 internal experience: around 180p wholesale, some suppliers previously withdrew books, suspended pricing and returned higher. That is the direct customer consequence.

Ceasefire Reality Check

Front by front

  • Fresh US attacks on Iran? No.
  • Fresh Israeli attacks on Iran? None verified overnight.
  • Fresh Iranian attacks? None verified overnight; Iran threatens shipping if hostilities resume.
  • US-Houthi involvement? Weighed, not executed.
  • Saudi strikes on Yemen? Continuing; Houthi-run authorities report civilian casualties in Sanaa.
  • Houthi attacks? Yes: Monday missile intercepted; airports struck repeatedly over the past week.
  • Hormuz? Restricted, not closed; crude moving, LNG weak.
  • Talks? Alive but stalled. Agreement? No.
  • Tanker attacks? Sporadic; LNG tanker incident near Hormuz reported 7 October.
  • LNG flow? Heavily constrained.

Reality

Trump's no-Iran-strike statement remains a de-escalation signal, but the wider regional war is still expanding through Yemen and Saudi Arabia. Talks are not normalisation. A pause on one front does not mean energy risk has disappeared on another.

The proposed Russia-Ukraine energy truce sits in the same category: announced measures to increase Russian diesel supply helped ease Brent, but Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure continued. A proposed truce is not a verified physical ceasefire.

Route normalisation matters more than political optimism. The physical test for UK gas is LNG volume through Hormuz, not the tone of the negotiations.

Shipping Risk

Hormuz: Oil

  • Crude flows materially recovered: ~16m bpd in September, ~80% of pre-war (Reuters/Kpler)
  • EIA: September regional shut-ins the lowest since hostilities began; convoys, bypass routes and STS transfers supporting flows
  • US-supported shipping continues; sporadic attacks and intimidation remain (UKMTO projectile reports)

Hormuz: LNG

  • 21 LNG cargoes exited in September (Kpler; S&P counted 19), the best month of the war but far below pre-war ~3 per day
  • Most carriers transit dark; specialist vessel willingness is the constraint, not just route access
  • Qatar force majeure extended; reporting indicates Asia and Europe deliveries affected through end-November
  • UAE cargoes also transiting, but volumes too low to move the global balance
Hormuz is working better for oil than LNG. Visible vessel count and physical energy volume are different measures.

Bab al-Mandeb

  • Houthis hold Yemen's Red Sea coastline, Perim and the Hanish Islands since mid-September
  • PLC forces claimed Mokha recaptured on 5 October; Houthis deny; fighting continues near the strait
  • ACLED: maritime targeting remains selective and focused on Saudi-linked shipping; capability to widen remains
  • Territorial control and shipping flow are different measures

Red Sea / Saudi Routes

  • Yanbu and the East-West Pipeline are Saudi Arabia's Hormuz bypass; the pipeline was attacked and closed for days last month, and the energy minister says more pipelines are being built
  • Saudi Red Sea crude exports ran roughly eight times 2025 levels between March and mid-July

Other Routes

  • Fujairah, Oman, Suez, SUMED and Cape routing all carrying diverted demand at higher cost
  • A route can remain physically open and still carry a material war-risk premium. Friction gets priced.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

WHOLESALE HAS MOVED. HAVE SUPPLIER BOOKS CAUGHT UP?

TTF settled up 3.6% on Monday, public NBP indications are above 200p, and Switch365's offered curve rose across every season this morning, led by W-26 gas at +6.80p. The chain now in motion:

  • Wholesale market moves → supplier book may lag → existing quote may remain temporarily available → supplier hedging catches up → book withdrawn or repriced → customer loses the earlier rate.

Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached approximately 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew books, suspended pricing and returned higher. That is internal commercial experience, not an official industry threshold, but the offered W-26 print of 200.01p sits well above it.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.
Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.
