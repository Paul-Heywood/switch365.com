Summary

Britain is deploying RAF Typhoon jets on defensive counter-air missions to protect Saudi cities and critical national infrastructure from Houthi missiles and drones, with an A400M transport aircraft and military advisers alongside existing air-defence and refuelling support. France is separately implementing strictly defensive support for Saudi energy infrastructure, with particular emphasis on Yanbu and the East-West pipeline corridor, the route that lets Saudi crude reach the Red Sea without passing through Hormuz.

Turkey moved its Mecca Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan to parliament for ratification on 20 October, while Egypt's parliament will vote on 26 October on authorising possible military support for Riyadh. Neither has deployed forces: Turkey's contribution is expected to centre on equipment, intelligence and weapons systems, and Egypt's request requires a two-thirds majority. The multinational picture is defensive preparation, not war entry.

Overnight the Houthis claimed six operations against Saudi oil, aviation and military targets, including Yanbu and the Shaybah oil field. Saudi authorities have not confirmed energy damage, and no verified Saudi production or export capacity loss exists this morning. The Houthi ballistic-missile attack in energy-rich Shabwa province, home to the Balhaf LNG complex, has now killed 73 people. Qatar LNG remains the biggest direct UK gas risk, with October tracking around 91% below pre-war, while Hormuz crude has recovered to roughly pre-war levels. Futures Market Explained

  • The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period.
  • A customer does not have to wait until their existing contract ends to see today's future-delivery price.
  • Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.
  • Backwardation does not mean wait. It means future delivery is cheaper today.
  • Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity.
  • Waiting is not neutral.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

WHOLESALE HAS MOVED. HAVE SUPPLIER BOOKS CAUGHT UP?

Internal Switch365 experience: when wholesale gas previously pushed around 180p/therm, some supplier books were withdrawn, suspended or returned higher. That is internal Switch365 commercial experience, not an official industry threshold. Wednesday's front-end easing to a 206.43p working reference does not remove that risk; it shows how quickly conditions can shift in both directions.

  • Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger.
  • Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.
  • Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.
  • The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.

If a customer holds a live, valid quote, review it against this morning's ranges immediately. Quote validity is the scarcest resource in a market where books can be withdrawn without notice. Secure where commercially appropriate; never blindly, never slowly.

Waiting Is Not Neutral

Waiting could help if:

  • international air defence reduces successful Houthi attacks
  • Yanbu, Shaybah and Balhaf remain unaffected
  • Qatar LNG improves and Hormuz stays open
  • Iran's mediation or Omani channels produce de-escalation
  • Norway and US LNG remain strong, weather stays mild, supplier books soften

Waiting could hurt if:

  • Houthi attacks penetrate defences
  • Yanbu loses capacity or the East-West Pipeline is disrupted again
  • Shaybah output falls or Balhaf is hit
  • an LNG vessel is hit, or Qatar LNG deteriorates further
  • supplier books rise or are withdrawn
WAITING ONLY WORKS IF THE MARKET MOVES IN THE CUSTOMER'S FAVOUR.
