Summary

What changed over the weekend that could change the price a UK business can secure today

Iran's parliament on Sunday approved veteran aerospace and missile official Mehrdad Akhlaqi Ketabchi as defence minister, with a mandate to rebuild the country's military-industrial infrastructure and accelerate defence technology development. He warned that another American attack would trigger a "surprising and extensive" response and said Iran has prepared measures to counter the potential use of the US GBU-43/B "Mother of All Bombs". On Friday, President Trump said the US "may have to" strike Iran's fortified Pickaxe Mountain nuclear facility, while ruling out nuclear weapons and ground troops. He did not confirm that the GBU-43/B had been authorised. This is direct presidential escalation rhetoric, not an attack order.

At the same time, Iran says its seven-condition proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz remains on the table. Foreign minister Abbas Araqchi said on Sunday the Strait could reopen within six days if Washington accepts the conditions, with negotiations restarting the following day. The core dispute is unresolved: Washington insists Iran's nuclear programme is addressed from the outset. The physical maritime situation is not behaving like a de-escalating conflict. UKMTO recorded two tanker attacks off Saudi Arabia on Friday, an armed approach against an LNG tanker near Yemen on Saturday and a tug struck by two projectiles near the UAE on Sunday. Egypt says it is ready to provide defensive military support to Saudi Arabia if requested and authorised, with parliamentary discussions continuing into Monday.

The physical energy comparison is unchanged and remains the heart of the story: oil through Hormuz is around 32% below pre-war, Qatar LNG was around 76% below pre-war in September, and October is currently tracking around 91% below pre-war. The war is hitting LNG far harder than oil. TTF is around €88/MWh and NBP front-month around 218 to 219 p/th on Monday morning, with Switch365 internal offered pricing moving up across the near curve. Brent is softer, near $108/bbl, on Trump's waiver of Russia-related sanctions, but Brent is not the customer's gas quote. Wholesale moved first. Supplier books may still follow.

Current Conditions

Verified position at Monday morning, 19 October 2026

Iran Military Preparation Escalating

New defence minister Ketabchi (approved Sunday, 237 votes for) pledges to rebuild military-industrial infrastructure and accelerate defence technology. He warned of a "surprising and extensive" response to any renewed US attack. Military preparation does not mean a strike is certain, but Iran is not behaving as though the possibility of renewed war has disappeared. Source: Reuters, operator statements.

US Strike Rhetoric Hardening

Trump said Friday the US "may have to" strike Pickaxe Mountain: "We'll knock it out fast." No nuclear weapon, no ground troops, no confirmed GBU-43/B authorisation. Competing signals: he previously signalled restraint before the midterms, yet is again publicly discussing future strikes. No verified force-posture change followed the remarks in this research run.

Hormuz Negotiations Stalled

Araqchi (Sunday, Baghdad): the seven-condition proposal is still on the table; the Strait could reopen on day six and talks resume on day seven if Washington accepts. Conditions include lifting the US blockade on Iranian ports, easing oil-export sanctions and ending hostilities on regional fronts. Washington insists the nuclear programme is addressed first. Offer alive. Talks stalled. No agreement. No implementation.

Hormuz Oil vs Pre-War ~-32%

Established Kpler comparison: ~11.62m bpd seven-day average vs ~17.13m bpd pre-war clearance baseline. Fresh Friday Kpler note (cited by Reuters Monday) shows Gulf crude flows fell sharply to about 8.5m bpd on a seven-day average last week, from above 20m bpd the prior week, after at least 10 confirmed vessel incidents. Different measure to Hormuz clearance, but the direction confirms renewed deterioration. This is oil flow, not vessel count.

Qatar LNG ~-76% Sept / ~-91% Oct track

September exports ~1.6m tonnes vs ~6.75m tonnes/month pre-war baseline. October currently tracking ~0.6m tonnes; the month is not complete. Ras Laffan remains heavily disrupted with around 17% of export capacity damaged. Qatar's emir last week publicly confirmed a "significant" portion of LNG exports is disrupted. Four laden Qatari cargoes crossed Hormuz around 2 to 3 October, and more tankers are entering the Gulf in ballast, but Qatar needs predictable safe passage, not occasional successful crossings.

LNG Shipping Security Worsening

An LNG tanker was approached 140NM southwest of Al Mukalla, Yemen on Saturday by a skiff with seven people onboard; the skiff fired two shots, the tanker's armed security team returned warning shots and the skiff disengaged. The vessel was not reported hit. An LNG tanker security incident matters even when the tanker is not physically struck. LNG shipping risk is not limited to Hormuz.

Saudi-Houthi Front Active

Ansar Allah, commonly called the Houthis, have attacked Riyadh's international airport and Saudi Aramco in recent weeks; 19 people have been killed and 364 injured in Saudi Arabia this month. The UK on Friday advised against all but essential travel to Saudi Arabia. Saudi-backed forces struck Houthi positions over the weekend. Threat is not the same as production loss: no confirmed Saudi energy capacity loss in this run.

Egypt Watch

President Sisi requested parliamentary authorisation to allow Egypt to help defend Saudi Arabia if the kingdom requests support. Sunday's emergency session adjourned; a second session is due Monday. Egypt sits alongside the Red Sea, Suez and SUMED. Potential Egyptian involvement can be both a security buffer and an escalation risk. Egypt has not entered the war.

Bab al-Mandeb Elevated

Houthi forces hold the Red Sea coastline including Mokha and reached Mayun island in the strait; a Houthi-declared blockade targets Saudi-affiliated shipping. Territorial control and physical shipping are different measures, but the chokepoint is under direct militia influence while Saudi-Houthi fighting escalates.

NBP Firm

UK front-month gas around 218 to 219 p/th Monday morning (public wholesale indication, CFD-tracked), up roughly 1.3% on the day and around 20% over the past month. ICE NBP Q1-27 last traded near 217.6 p/th on Friday. Public wholesale explains why. Switch365 internal offered pricing tells us what the customer can buy.

TTF Firm

Dutch TTF benchmark around €88/MWh Monday, up ~0.9% on the day and ~21% over the month, at its highest since the early war period. Europe is pricing the weekend escalation more than the Russia sanctions relief. TTF is the transmission hub between Qatar LNG loss and NBP.

European Storage ~74%

GIE AGSI+ reads around 74.2 to 74.3% full (gas days 15 to 18 October), versus a five-year norm near 87% for this point in October. The relaxed 80% November target is borderline at current injection pace. Storage is a buffer, not immunity. Europe does not need to run out of gas for low storage to raise prices.

Norway Cushion

No fresh major Norwegian outage was verified in this research run. Norway remains Europe's largest pipeline supplier and materially cushions missing LNG. Norway matters more when Qatar LNG is 75% to 90% below pre-war. Gassco nominations remain the daily check.

US LNG Buffer

US LNG is Europe's most important flexible LNG buffer, with record export capacity operational and Europe the largest destination. No fresh terminal outage was verified over the weekend. It cannot automatically replace all lost Qatar LNG: Europe and Asia are bidding for the same Atlantic cargoes.

Brent Softer

Brent fell around 1.8% to roughly $108/bbl early Monday after Trump waived key Russia-related sanctions and tariffs under the Graham Act, following a diesel export deal with Putin (about 2.3m barrels this month, rising towards 7.6m monthly). Brent touched $110 on Friday, its highest since May. Maritime risk can worsen while Brent falls: Brent is not a reliable proxy for Qatar LNG disruption.

Supplier Books Lagging

Switch365 internal offered gas and power ranges both moved up across the near curve on Monday morning. Internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180 p/th, some suppliers pulled books, paused pricing and returned higher. Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger.

Customer Action Review Now

Futures Market Explained

Why today's forward curve is a live commercial opportunity, not a forecast

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their existing contract ends to see today's future-delivery price. The curve is currently in steep backwardation: winter 2026 energy is priced far above 2028, 2029 and 2030 delivery.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.
Backwardation does not mean wait. It means that future delivery is cheaper today.

Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. If the physical disruption normalises, the front of the curve may fall, but the far curve can also reprice upwards on any renewed escalation, supplier withdrawal or cold-weather shock. Waiting is not neutral.

British Gas 18-Month Window

Verified Switch365 internal evidence

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing. The contract does not start today. It begins when the existing agreement ends.

The 18-month window turns today's forward curve into something customers may actually be able to secure. With S-27 and W-27 sitting roughly 23% to 27% below W-26 on gas and around 23% below on power, eligibility is the difference between watching the discount and capturing it.

Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.

British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action. Not every customer is automatically eligible; the check takes minutes and the window is the widest currently on the market.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

Wholesale has moved. Have supplier books caught up?

Wholesale gas has risen roughly 20% over the past month and the internal near curve moved again this morning. Switch365 internal commercial experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180 p/th, some suppliers pulled books, paused pricing and returned higher. Today's W-26 working reference is 214.79 p/th. Books are open today, but the direction of travel is clear.

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.
Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger.
  • Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.
  • The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow: a quote issued last week can already be below replacement cost.
  • An existing valid quote is a wasting asset. Its value decays as books reprice.
