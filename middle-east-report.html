SECTION 01

Summary

Overnight picture

Iran is widening its maritime campaign beyond the Strait of Hormuz. Two senior Iranian officials told Reuters that Tehran changed its offensive tactics against shipping roughly ten days ago and intends to expand operations into the Gulf of Oman and waters near Qatar and the UAE. At least five commercial-shipping incidents were recorded in roughly 48 hours, and a US military official confirmed Iran has begun using small civilian-style vessels as spotters after losing radar capability. One official's stated intention was that "it will be complete closure soon". That is an Iranian official's stated intention, not a verified present-day total closure.

Qatar LNG is the pressure point. The question is no longer only whether energy can get through Hormuz; it is whether Gulf energy can safely reach the open sea after it clears Hormuz. Qatar exported only around 1.6 million tonnes of LNG in September, less than a quarter of pre-war normal, with October tracking near 0.6 million tonnes and force majeure extended. Oil has adapted to the war far better than LNG: crude enjoys pipeline bypasses and a huge flexible tanker fleet, while LNG depends on specialised carriers, liquefaction and predictable safe passage. Hormuz working for crude does not mean it is working for LNG.

A Saudi Aramco administrative building at its Dhahran headquarters complex was struck late Tuesday in a Houthi ballistic-missile attack, with a limited fire and no injuries, but no physical oil-supply loss has yet been confirmed. Switch365 internal offered data shows W-26 gas at 209.00p/therm (+8.99 on the day) and W-26 power at £160.27/MWh (+£6.89), while Brent trades near $106/bbl. Wholesale has moved first. Supplier books often follow. For customers, the practical consequence is simple: this is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market.

What It Means for UK Energy

The direct UK gas link runs through Qatar LNG into TTF and then NBP, not through Brent. With Qatar severely impaired, Europe entering winter at roughly 74% storage and supplier books sensitive to further wholesale gains, customers holding a valid quote, or within a supplier renewal window, should compare today's replacement price against what they can still secure now. Waiting is not neutral.

SECTION 05

Futures Market Explained

Why the curve matters
What a Future Period Is

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their current contract ends to see today's price for future delivery. W-26 is winter 2026, S-27 is summer 2027, and so on through the seasons to 2030.

What Backwardation Means

The curve is heavily backwardated: near delivery is expensive because the war premium sits in the front, and later delivery is cheaper today. Backwardation does not mean wait. It means the future delivery period is cheaper today.

Why Cheaper Further Out Is Not Cheaper Later

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. It means the future period is cheaper now. The question is whether the customer can secure that future period now, while supplier windows allow it.

Why Waiting Is Not Free

Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. If the war premium spreads along the curve, or a supplier reprices its book, the future period being cheap today stops mattering. Waiting is not neutral.

British Gas 18-Month Window

Switch365 internal evidence
What Changed

British Gas has extended its business-energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing. The contract does not start today: it begins when the existing contract ends.

Why It Matters Today

The 18-month window matters because it can make today's forward-value gap actionable. A customer whose contract end date (CED) falls inside 18 months can now test S-27 and W-27 pricing, sitting 24 to 29% below W-26 on gas, instead of waiting for a renewal panic inside 12 months.

Commercial Connection

British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action. Eligibility is not automatic for every customer, and supplier availability can change without notice: once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. Check eligibility and live pricing now, compare it against the actual CED, and let the curve decide.

SECTION 15

Shipping Risk

Route by route

Hormuz

Hormuz is not open as usual and is not completely closed. The current system mixes Iranian restrictions, attacks, surveillance, US naval protection, some large oil flows, limited LNG movement and high shipping risk. Around 90 maritime incidents involving commercial vessels have been recorded across the Gulf, Hormuz and Gulf of Oman since the war began, with 24 seafarer deaths reported to the IMO. Visible vessel count and physical energy volume are different measures: roughly 28 US-facilitated transits a day are getting through, against about 138 a day before the war.

Gulf of Oman

The Gulf of Oman is now an active risk area, not a safe exit. On 11 October a drone hit a Nicaragua-flagged tanker near the Sohar anchorage, leaving two Indian seafarers missing, and UKMTO issued an advisory for an incident involving a tanker and military forces. Senior Iranian officials told Reuters operations are being expanded into these waters. That is a higher-risk designation, not confirmed closure: do not treat Gulf of Oman risk as the same as confirmed closure.

Qatar / UAE Waters

A tanker was attacked off the Qatar coast on 8 October, and on 10 October shipmasters near Ras Al Khaimah received VHF messages ordering them to leave anchorages. These waters are part of the system designed to reduce Hormuz dependence: Fujairah, the eastern export terminals and the Qatar LNG approaches. If Iran expands pressure here, the workaround itself becomes part of the risk.

Bab al-Mandeb

The Houthis retain missile and drone capability against Red Sea routes, and the Saudi coalition is striking their fuel and launch infrastructure. Territorial control and shipping flow are different measures: coastal gains by Saudi-backed forces do not make Bab al-Mandeb safe.

Red Sea / Saudi

The East-West Pipeline and Yanbu remain the kingdom's strategic bypass. The pipeline was temporarily shut last month after an attack launched from Iraq, and a Red Sea export port has been targeted. EIA cites the East-West attacks as a source of continued volatility in physical oil flows.

The New Risk Model

The risk is no longer just whether Hormuz stays open. Iran is now threatening the wider route network used to move Gulf energy to market. Old model: Hormuz, then open sea. New model: Hormuz, then the Gulf of Oman, then the waters approaching Qatar and the UAE, then open sea. Clearing Hormuz may no longer mean clearing the danger zone. A route can remain physically open and still carry a material war-risk premium. Friction gets priced.

SECTION 17

Supplier Price-Book Lag

The opportunity and the danger

WHOLESALE HAS MOVED. HAVE SUPPLIER BOOKS CAUGHT UP?

Switch365 morning data shows the offered ranges already moving: W-26 gas +8.99p and W-26 power +£6.89 in a single snapshot. The chain runs like this: wholesale moves, the supplier book may remain temporarily unchanged, the supplier hedge cost catches up, the book is repriced or withdrawn, the old rate becomes unavailable, and the customer pays the replacement price.

Switch365 Internal Experience

When wholesale gas previously reached approximately 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew books, suspended pricing and returned higher. That is Switch365 internal commercial experience, not an industry-wide formal trigger. W-26 now stands at 209.00p.

What To Do With It

If a quote predates the latest wholesale move and remains valid, it may be priced off a market that no longer exists. Compare it immediately with today's replacement price while the lag lasts.

Locked Wording

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.
