5Futures Market Explained

Why a supplier can quote 2028 gas at less than half today's winter reference

Wholesale gas and power trade as contracts for specific future delivery periods: seasons (summer/winter), quarters, months and years. Each has its own price, set by expected supply and demand for that period, not by today's spot conditions alone. When near-term prices sit above later-dated prices, the curve is in backwardation; the reverse is contango.

Today's Switch365 table is a offered-price curve: W-26 gas at 223.86p against S-28 at 91.50p is steep backwardation. It tells you what the market currently charges for each delivery window. It does not tell you what gas will cost in 2028, and it is not a whole-bill discount: customer tariffs also carry standing charges, non-commodity costs, margins and credit terms.

Four different price types appear in this report and must not be conflated: public ICE exchange prices (NBP/TTF futures), Switch365 seasonal offered indicators, the W-26 working reference (user-supplied for gas, modelled for power), and an actual executable customer quote. Only the last of these is a price a business can sign.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. Backwardation does not mean wait.

This is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market. Waiting is not neutral.

8British Gas 18-Month Window

An extended renewal runway that turns curve intelligence into a practical action

British Gas allows eligible business customers to agree a renewal up to 18 months before their current contract ends. The new agreement still starts on the existing contract end date; the window only changes when you can lock the price, not when supply switches. Eligibility is not automatic and must be confirmed per customer, per meter.

In a backwardated market this matters twice over. First, a customer inside the window can test today's forward prices instead of waiting for a renewal quote that arrives after supplier books have caught up with wholesale. Second, the gap between near-term and later-delivery seasons (S-28 gas at 59.1% below the W-26 comparator, on offered indicators) is exactly the kind of structure worth pricing against a real, dated, customer-specific offer.

What to check today: whether the customer sits inside the 18-month window; which products and terms British Gas will actually quote for that meter and consumption band; the full unit rate and standing charge, not the seasonal indicator; and the quote's validity period. British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action.

Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.

14Ceasefire Reality Check

Diplomatic contact is not a ceasefire, and a ceasefire announcement is not verified safe passage

There is no operative signed ceasefire. What exists this Monday: a G7 condemnation statement dated Sunday 15 Nov, Iranian signals that diplomacy remains possible, a US president saying further strikes may not be necessary, and, pulling the other way, reported US-Israeli contingency planning for strikes on Iranian economic and energy infrastructure and a third US carrier arriving in theatre. None of that is a signed, enforceable agreement, and none of it is a verified safe maritime corridor.

The physical tests remain unchanged: have UKMTO and operators confirmed repeatable safe LNG and oil tanker passage through Hormuz, the Gulf of Aden and Bab al-Mandeb? Have Qatar's loadings risen sustainably rather than in a single strong month? Is Norwegian gas flowing after the Troll and Visund outages? Is European storage still in net withdrawal? Is falling Brent actually arriving with lower TTF and NBP and softer supplier books, or is it only oil-market relief?

October's shipping data show partial recovery, not normalisation: 31 laden LNG exits through Hormuz (S&P Global Energy) against a pre-war rhythm of roughly three LNG shipments a day. An improvement in security can reduce a risk premium before damaged infrastructure is repaired, but physical flows are the supply test. Never assume price direction from the geopolitical narrative alone.

LNG remains the bridge between Gulf security and UK gas. For UK gas, watch actual LNG, TTF and NBP rather than Brent alone.

17Supplier Price-Book Lag

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.

The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow: supplier books reprice after wholesale moves, and the lag between the two is where customer outcomes are decided. Monday's evidence is genuinely mixed, which makes the lag cut both ways. Near-term references firmed (+1.87% on the W-26 gas comparator), which argues for checking whether Friday's near-term and 2027 offers still stand. But seven of eight later-delivery gas seasons fell, which argues for actively pulling 2028–2030 future-start pricing before books catch up in either direction.

What counts as evidence of a book move: a named supplier, a dated quote, contract start, term, kWh band, standing charge, unit rate, pass-through conditions, credit and expiry. What does not count: inferring repricing from Brent, from the W-26 proxy, or from a headline. No supplier withdrawal or increase has been independently confirmed in this run, and no quote expiry should be invented.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.
