05 | Procurement literacy

Futures Market Explained

What the curve means

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their existing contract ends to see today's future-delivery price.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. Backwardation means future delivery is cheaper today. It does not guarantee that waiting will produce a lower quote.

What the customer should do

Backwardation does not mean wait. Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.

Review live future-start pricing against the real contract end date, supplier window and eligibility. A forward market indication becomes commercially useful only when a supplier offers a suitable, valid product to that customer.

Today's indicative pricing for 2029 delivery is substantially below the near-term working reference. That is a current curve relationship, not a prediction that prices will be cheaper in 2029.

14 | Operational tests, not headlines

Ceasefire Reality Check

Security and infrastructure

  • Did Tuesday's reported interceptions prevent meaningful energy damage, and did Ras Tanura suffer any independently verified refinery or export consequence?
  • Are Shaybah, Ras Tanura, Abqaiq, Dhahran, East-West, Yanbu and Rabigh operating with measured output or loading evidence?
  • Has Egypt moved beyond legal authority to a Saudi-requested, verified deployment?
  • Are UK, French and Turkish defensive claims supported by actual operational activity?

Diplomacy and physical supply

  • Have Tehran, Moscow and Gulf contacts produced a signed, enforceable ceasefire or only further dialogue?
  • Do UKMTO and ship operators confirm repeatable safe LNG and oil-tanker passage through Hormuz, Gulf of Aden and Bab al-Mandeb?
  • Have Qatari loadings and export tonnage risen sustainably, or are buyers still sourcing replacement cargoes?
  • Are Norway and US LNG physically strong, and is any oil-price decline matched by TTF, NBP and supplier books?

Diplomatic contact is not a ceasefire, and a ceasefire announcement is not verified safe passage.

An intercepted missile is not a confirmed energy infrastructure hit, and a hit is not necessarily lost production.

An improvement in security can reduce a risk premium before damaged infrastructure is repaired, but physical flows are the supply test.

15 | Route-by-route physical and insurance risk

Shipping Risk

The security risk zone is not confined to Hormuz

Hormuz

A conditional reopening proposal is not operational confirmation. No reconciled current oil-clearance baseline is published. Test named LNG departures, repeat safe passage and insurance rather than a single crossing.

Gulf of Oman

Earlier tanker incidents near the Hormuz approaches remain relevant background. No new named 27–28 October vessel casualty was independently verified in this run. Vessel identity, cargo and laden status must precede market inference.

Persian Gulf

Iranian seven-vessel claims are not treated as seven confirmed losses. Cross-check UKMTO, operators, trackers, flag states and insurers vessel by vessel, without double-counting strike and aftermath.

Gulf of Aden

Shabwa security risk remains relevant because the province contains Balhaf LNG, but the earlier attack did not prove Balhaf damage or supply loss. Current port and loading evidence is required.

Bab al-Mandeb

Houthi capability keeps the route under review. Separate attacks, approaches, diversions and actual passage interruption. An LNG security incident matters more to UK gas than an unrelated general-cargo event.

Red Sea

As Hormuz becomes less reliable, the Red Sea route becomes more valuable and strategic. Rerouting and insurance effects can persist even where cargo volumes continue.

Yanbu / Red Sea Corridor

Saudi crude can move from eastern fields through East-West to Yanbu, reducing Hormuz reliance. Recent claims require current pumping and loading evidence. Do not assert a fresh outage without measured flows.

LNG Carriers

Qatar needs predictable safe passage, not occasional successful crossings. Require multiple loadings, rising tonnage, customer deliveries and easing force majeure before calling recovery.

Tanker War-Risk Insurance

A route can remain open and still carry a substantial war-risk premium. Friction gets priced. Freight and insurance can raise delivered cost without an equivalent loss of physical production.

17 | Supplier transmission

Wholesale Has Moved. Have Supplier Books Caught Up?

The opportunity

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. A valid existing offer may not yet reflect the latest market direction, creating a review opportunity where the product suits the customer.

Switch365 internal experience shows that offered periods can move by different amounts. The latest W-27 gas reduction and W-27 power increase illustrate why each delivery period must be checked separately.

The danger

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. When wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, Switch365 experienced some books being withdrawn, suspended or returning higher. This is internal commercial experience, not an official market trigger.

Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.
