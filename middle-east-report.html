1. Summary

What changed overnight that could change the price a UK business can actually secure today

A drone targeting Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield was intercepted on Wednesday, but falling debris killed two workers and injured two more. The Saudi Ministry of Energy says the drone was aimed at a processing facility and that debris fell outside the perimeter, setting fire to a contractor's vehicle. This is the first verified physical attack on the Shaybah producing area, a field of roughly one million barrels per day, but operations were reported to be continuing and no production loss has been established. Hours later, King Fahd International Airport in Dammam was hit, killing one person and injuring eight, demonstrating continued Houthi strike capability inside Saudi Arabia's eastern energy heartland, close to Dhahran, Ras Tanura, Jubail, Abqaiq and Khurais.

Governments are now physically organising the defence of Saudi Arabia. Britain has announced RAF Typhoon defensive counter-air missions to protect Saudi cities and critical national infrastructure, France is deploying personnel, radars and air-defence systems around Yanbu and the East-West corridor, Turkey is preparing a parliamentary mandate that could allow air-defence and electronic-warfare personnel to deploy under the Mecca defence pact, and President Sisi says Egypt is prepared to defend Saudi territory, with a parliamentary vote set for 26 October. The US ordered departure of non-emergency personnel is a further security signal, not evidence of lost energy capacity.

For UK customers, the transmission channel remains LNG, shipping and the forward curve, not the headlines alone. A tanker has been struck near the Hormuz approaches for the second consecutive day, Iran claims seven vessels have been hit or mined, Qatar LNG is tracking around 91% below pre-war in October, TTF front month settled at €87.43/MWh and NBP front month is changing hands around 213 to 215p/therm, near four-year highs. Switch365 internal offered prices show the near-term working reference moving up again this morning while 2028 to 2030 delivery remains 46% to 66% below it. Supplier books lag wholesale, which is precisely where the opportunity and the danger sit.

5. Futures Market Explained

Why the forward curve matters to a customer whose contract has not ended yet

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their existing contract ends to see today's future-delivery price.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. Backwardation does not mean wait. It means future delivery is cheaper today. Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.

The % vs W-26 column shows the current forward-value gap: how much cheaper each future delivery period is, priced today, relative to the near-term working reference.
The 1 Year Ago line shows how much the market has moved, but the latest line is the customer's decision point. A year ago S-27 gas was 71.00p; today it is 168.50p.

8. British Gas 18-Month Window

Verified Switch365 internal evidence

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing.

The contract does not start today. It begins when the existing agreement ends. The 18-month window turns today's forward curve into something customers may actually be able to secure.

British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action. A customer whose contract ends in, for example, spring 2028 can potentially look at today's S-28 and W-28 pricing, around 47% to 53% below the near-term reference, rather than waiting for the renewal letter to arrive in a market that may have re-priced.

Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. Eligibility is not automatic for every customer; check it against the live book today.

10. Top Five Warning Signs

The five developments that would change this report's commercial conclusion
  • 1. SHAYBAH SUFFERS VERIFIED PRODUCTION OR PROCESSING LOSS. Confirmation from Aramco or the Saudi Energy Ministry that barrels or processing capacity are actually offline.
  • 2. YANBU OR THE EAST-WEST PIPELINE SUFFERS VERIFIED CAPACITY LOSS. The Hormuz bypass is Saudi Arabia's pressure valve; losing it would re-rate crude and LNG risk simultaneously.
  • 3. AN LNG CARRIER OR BALHAF LNG IS PHYSICALLY HIT. An LNG security incident matters more to UK gas than an unrelated general-cargo incident.
  • 4. QATAR LNG OR HORMUZ FLOWS DETERIORATE FURTHER. October is already tracking ~91% below pre-war; any further loss of safe, repeatable passage tightens Europe's LNG balance into winter.
  • 5. TTF / NBP RISE AND SUPPLIERS WITHDRAW OR REPRICE BOOKS. The moment wholesale escalation becomes a supplier-book event is the moment customers lose today's rates.

17. Supplier Price-Book Lag

WHOLESALE HAS MOVED. HAVE SUPPLIER BOOKS CAUGHT UP?

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. TTF has risen more than 20% since the end of September and NBP front month is near four-year highs; supplier books re-price on their own timetable, not the exchange's.

Switch365 internal commercial experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, some supplier books were withdrawn, were suspended, or returned higher. That was internal experience, not an official market trigger. With the near-term working reference now at 213.11p/therm, the conditions that previously produced withdrawals are comfortably exceeded.

The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow. A book re-priced tonight applies Wednesday and Thursday's wholesale move even if Friday is calm.
Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. An existing valid quote held this morning is an asset with an expiry time.

Final Commercial Conclusion

The Saudi energy story has now moved from Houthi threats and claims to a verified physical attack on the Shaybah oilfield, with two people killed. However, no verified production loss has yet been established, so the critical market test is whether the attack removed actual barrels from the market. At the same time, Dammam has been hit, another tanker has been struck near Hormuz, Iran claims seven vessels have been hit or mined, and Britain, France, Turkey and Egypt are moving to strengthen Saudi defence. Qatar LNG remains the most important direct transmission channel into UK gas. The commercial question is what this physical escalation does to TTF, NBP, supplier books and the price a UK business can actually secure today.

Saudi energy infrastructure is now under verified physical attack. Attack confirmed. Capacity loss not confirmed. The market cares about the attack, but it cares even more about whether barrels were removed. For UK gas, watch LNG, not just Brent.
