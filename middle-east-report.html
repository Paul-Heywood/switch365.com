Summary

US Central Command says American forces destroyed five Iranian crude-oil tankers on Tuesday 8 September, four in the Gulf of Oman and one near Kharg Island, after the IRGC attempted to hit a US Navy warship with ballistic missiles twice over the previous two days. Crews were directed to abandon ship before the vessels were struck. This is the clearest sign yet that US economic pressure on Iran is becoming increasingly physical: oil in the ground does not fund Iran. Oil that can be sold and paid for does.

Iran retaliated within hours. Tehran launched 20 ballistic missiles towards the US-used Al Azraq base in Jordan; Jordan says 18 were intercepted, two fell in unpopulated areas and no casualties were reported, while a US official called the attack ineffective. The IRGC separately claims it targeted two US vessels and eight oil tankers in the Gulf and attacked ten ships attempting to transit Hormuz. That remains an Iranian claim, not an independently verified event. A maritime-security source also reports an LNG tanker damaged at Khor Fakkan in the UAE, on the Gulf of Oman side outside the Strait itself, with responsibility not yet established.

For UK business energy the transmission is already visible. Kpler tracked just six commodity vessels through Hormuz on Tuesday, five entering and only one exiting, against a 10-day average of around 12. UK quarterly NBP closed Tuesday at 193.26p/therm, up roughly 4.7% on the day and a new 52-week high, and Switch365 internal indications show W-26 already at around 195p/therm this morning. Yesterday's internal offered range, detailed below, has not yet caught up with this morning's wholesale move. That gap is the opportunity and the danger.

Section 05

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their contract end date to see today's price for future delivery. When the offered range shows Summer 2027 gas at 125.50p while Winter 2026 sits at 188.25p (and near 195p this morning), that gap is a live, tradable number today, not a forecast of what 2027 will cost later.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. The customer mistake is thinking a cheaper future period means they should wait. It does not. It means we should check whether that future period can be secured now. Backwardation does not mean wait. The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period, and waiting gives the market time to remove today's forward-value opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.

British Gas 18-Month Window

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier chance to review future-start pricing. The 18-month British Gas window matters because it turns the forward market into a practical customer action: with the curve in steep backwardation, a customer whose contract ends within 18 months can now test pricing for delivery periods sitting 30% to 60% below today's 195p front market.

  • Your new contract would not start today. It would begin when your current agreement ends.
  • Waiting is not neutral. If the market moves or British Gas withdraws rates, customers cannot go back and secure today's pricing later.
  • Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. It means check whether the future period can be secured now.
  • Not every customer is automatically eligible; eligibility and live pricing need to be checked site by site.
British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action. With near-term gas around the 190p to 195p area and Winter 2028 offered at 87.00p on yesterday's sheet, the gap between acting inside the window and waiting for renewal panic has rarely been wider.

Market Drivers

The hero is the freshest material change; this ranking is what matters most to UK gas and power prices today.

1. Qatar LNG / Hormuz BULLISH

The main physical link between Hormuz and UK gas. QatarEnergy's force majeure, declared after the March strikes on Ras Laffan, has now cancelled 21 Edison cargoes due between April and September and runs into the autumn; around 17% of Qatari capacity (12.8 mtpa) is offline for years. Kpler tracked six commodity vessels through Hormuz on Tuesday. Confidence: high. UK businesses should care because every missing Qatari cargo tightens the global LNG pool the UK buys from.

2. NBP / TTF BULLISH

Quarterly NBP closed Tuesday at 193.26p/therm, a new 52-week high, up roughly 15.5% since 28 August, with internal W-26 indications around 195p this morning. TTF indicated near €77.33/MWh, up about 27% in a month. Confidence: high on direction; internal figures override public indications for customer pricing.

3. European Storage BULLISH

EU storage around 66%, Germany 54.75% as of 7 September (AGSI), the weakest for this point of the year in around 15 years, and roughly 19 percentage points below last year. Germany's storage sector warns of insufficiency in an extremely cold winter, while the Economy Ministry points to diversified import infrastructure. Both are true: storage is a buffer, not immunity.

4. Norway BEARISH BUFFER

Norway remains one of the strongest cushions against LNG disruption and is now Germany's largest supplier. September is the heavy maintenance month, so nominations deserve daily watching, but no major unplanned outage was identified in this run. Strong Norwegian supply materially cushions missing LNG.

5. US / Replacement LNG BEARISH BUFFER

US LNG remains one of the strongest bearish buffers against Qatar disruption, with feedgas near record levels and no major plant outage reported. A wide JKM premium over TTF continues to pull some spot cargoes towards Asia, which limits how much relief reaches Europe.

6. Hormuz Shipping / Commercial Tanker Risk BULLISH

UKMTO rates the Strait of Hormuz SEVERE with further deliberate hostile action likely. Iran's claimed attacks on ten vessels are unverified, but the visible count, dark shipping and the 5-in/1-out imbalance keep the war-risk premium entrenched. Uncertainty itself can carry a risk premium.

7. Supplier Price Books MIXED

Internal Switch365 experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew books, suspended pricing and reissued at higher rates. The market has moved well beyond that internal warning level. Books open this morning may not be open this afternoon.

8. US Economic Pressure / Iran Retaliation BULLISH, TWO-SIDED

The pressure can cut both ways. Bullish escalation case: a squeezed Iran takes greater military risk. Bearish diplomatic case: confrontation becomes economically unsustainable and Iran compromises. Fresh evidence, missiles at Jordan and claims against shipping, currently points towards escalation rather than compromise.

9. Saudi / Houthi Energy Risk BULLISH

The Iran-aligned Houthis have expanded attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure, with strikes around Abha, Najran and Jazan causing fires and temporary shutdowns this week. If Hormuz is the problem, Saudi and Red Sea infrastructure are part of the workaround, and that workaround is now under direct pressure.

10. UK Power Fundamentals MIXED

Wind is running below seasonal norms early this week, UK nuclear is in a planned outage window and French availability is off its 2025 highs, all keeping gas plant marginal. Interconnector flows remain healthy, the main offset.

11. Brent MIXED

Brent near $99.50/bbl, a fourth consecutive gain, confirms the regional risk premium. But Brent is not the customer's gas quote: UK business gas analysis centres on NBP, TTF, LNG, storage, Norway and supplier books. The oil system is damaged but adapting, with alternative routes and non-OPEC supply preventing an even higher price.

The immediate UK consequence. Internal indications already show W-26 around 195p this morning; a sustained move through 200p with book withdrawals would convert the wholesale story into an unavoidable customer price rise.

5. The Saudi / Houthi or US / Iran exchange escalates again

Especially against energy infrastructure. A confirmed Saudi strike campaign into Yemen, or further US strikes on Iranian tankers answered with verified attacks on commercial shipping, would narrow the system's remaining security margin.

Section 15

Shipping Risk

Hormuz: severely impaired

  • Kpler tracked six commodity vessels through Hormuz on Tuesday (preliminary), down from nine on Monday and a 10-day average of around 12
  • Of Tuesday's six: five entered and only one exited. Do not read this automatically as trapped ships; waiting, delayed exits, dark movements and loading schedules can all contribute, but a growing in-Gulf backlog would be important
  • Only two vessels transited on Saturday, the lowest daily count of the recent run
  • Measurement itself is now disputed: US officials have claimed more than 17 million bpd (and, this week, ~9 million bpd) still moving, while Kpler estimates crude flows around 5 million bpd in early September. We do not currently know Hormuz physical flow with the precision we once did
  • UKMTO threat rating: SEVERE; further deliberate hostile action likely
Hormuz is not open as usual. Hormuz remains severely impaired, with unusually poor visibility over the true level of physical energy movement.

Iranian tankers + Iran's claims

  • Five IRGC crude carriers destroyed Tuesday: four in the Gulf of Oman, one near Kharg Island; one identified as the M/T Riesco
  • Three more destroyed on 5 September (Downy, Stark 1, Kylo/Noxen), eight in four days
  • Five tankers alone do not stop Iranian exports, but they tighten an already restricted export system: sanctions, blockade, buyer risk, vessel scarcity, financing, insurance and routing all compound
  • Iran claims attacks on two US vessels and eight oil tankers, plus ten ships near Hormuz that it says entered a "prohibited and unsafe" area. This is an Iranian claim; verification via UKMTO, operators and trackers is pending
  • Iran has also threatened tankers in Kuwaiti and Bahraini ports, potentially extending risk beyond the narrowest part of Hormuz. Rhetoric is not promoted to disruption until evidence appears

LNG Khor Fakkan + Qatar LNG

  • An LNG tanker has reportedly been damaged at Khor Fakkan, but responsibility remains unclear; the crew was reported safe and early indications suggest limited damage
  • Khor Fakkan sits on the Gulf of Oman side, outside the Strait. If LNG shipping there starts being targeted, the risk spreads into the workaround corridors themselves
  • Qatar: force majeure since March, 21 Edison cargoes cancelled April to September, disruption managed over months, not days. Rare LNG ship-to-ship transfers outside Hormuz show resilience and disruption at the same time
  • LPG movement is not LNG normalisation; propane and butane cargoes prove nothing about methane supply

Bab al-Mandab + workarounds

  • Kpler tracked 25 commodity vessels through Bab al-Mandab on Tuesday, near the 10-day average of about 27, including Suezmax, Aframax and a VLCC
  • Transits are down roughly 26% since the 20 July Houthi blockade declaration (UKMTO), and JMIC rates the southern Red Sea SUBSTANTIAL, but the strait continues to handle substantial commercial traffic
  • Saudi East-West pipeline to Yanbu, UAE eastern routes, SUMED/Sidi Kerir and the Cape all remain functioning alternatives; Saudi-linked loadings increasingly run dark
Bab al-Mandab carries elevated military risk but remains physically much closer to normal than Hormuz. A route can remain physically open and still carry a material war-risk premium. Friction gets priced.

The energy system still has workarounds, but their security margin is narrower. Visible vessel count and physical energy volume are different measures: low visible traffic is evidence of severe disruption, not proof that nothing is moving. We no longer have the same visibility over Hormuz flows that the market had before the war.

Wholesale has already moved. The question now is when supplier books catch up. Internal Switch365 experience shows that when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew books, suspended pricing and reissued at higher rates. That is internal commercial evidence, not a formal industry threshold, and the market has now moved well beyond that level.

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. Yesterday's offered range (188.25p W-26) was printed before Tuesday's 193.26p close and roughly 7p below this morning's 195p indication. Any supplier still honouring Monday or Tuesday pricing is, in effect, selling yesterday's market. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. Check the actual supplier book.

Existing quote argument

If a customer holds a quote created before the move through 190p, check whether it remains valid: supplier, validity window, acceptance deadline, term and the current replacement quote. The wholesale market has moved materially since that price was produced. If the supplier is still prepared to honour it, it deserves serious review because there is no guarantee the book remains available. Not every old quote is automatically good; compare it with live supplier pricing first.

Customer Action

The physical and geopolitical risk has increased at the same time UK gas has moved to a fresh 52-week high and through 195p this morning. The commercial question is whether suppliers have fully reflected that move yet. The action everywhere is to review live pricing now, never to contract blindly.

1. Existing pre-surge quote · TOP PRIORITY

If the price was produced before the move through 190p and remains valid, compare it immediately with today's live book. Check supplier, validity, current book, acceptance deadline, term and the current replacement quote.

2. Contract ends in 2026 · Immediate review

For customers ending this year, waiting is becoming an increasingly concentrated gamble. Near-term delivery is exactly where the war premium sits: W-26 at 188.25p yesterday, ~195p this morning, against 87.20p a year ago.

3. Inside 12 months · Do not wait for renewal panic

Check today's future-start pricing against the actual contract end date. S-27 gas at 125.50p and power at £102.00 remain a third below the front, but both rose a full point yesterday and will not wait for a diary reminder.

4. British Gas inside 18 months · Extended renewal window

Check eligibility and live pricing now. The extended window gives an earlier chance to secure future-start pricing while the curve remains in backwardation. The contract begins when the current agreement ends, not today.

5. 2027 / 2028 / 2029 / 2030 · Your delivery period matters

Compare Latest, % vs W-26 and 1 Year Ago for the actual delivery period, and confirm supplier availability. S-29 power is below its year-ago level and W-29 gas is only 0.52p above last year: genuine forward value, but only while a supplier still offers those periods.

Waiting is not neutral

Waiting can benefit the customer if wholesale falls or suppliers reduce books. Waiting can cost the customer if wholesale rises, a quote expires, or a supplier withdraws or reprices. Waiting is a market decision, and waiting only works if the market moves in the customer's favour.
