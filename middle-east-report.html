Section 01

Summary

Hormuz is no longer just a risk story. It is now a physical flow story. Kpler tracked only seven commodity-vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz on 6 October, the lowest since 23 July, recovering only slightly to around ten on Wednesday after more than twenty a day had been crossing earlier in the week. Crude flow through the strait has fallen approximately 27% to about 10.1 million barrels per day, roughly 74% of pre-war volumes, following the heaviest run of tanker attacks since the Iran war began, including at least twelve attacks, attempted attacks and harassment events in the week to 5 October.

The counterpoint matters as much as the headline. This is not a supply collapse. Oil exports using Gulf of Oman and Red Sea routes have risen to approximately 6.7 million barrels per day, more than double their pre-war level, and Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline is pumping around 5.8 million barrels per day to Yanbu. Overall Middle East crude exports remain close to pre-war levels. Hormuz has deteriorated, but the wider export system is compensating. Visible vessel count and physical energy volume are different measures, and a route can remain physically open and still carry a material war-risk premium. Friction gets priced.

For UK gas, the decisive next question is LNG, not crude. LNG remains the bridge between Hormuz and UK gas: Hormuz, to Qatar LNG, to global LNG availability, to Europe-Asia competition, to TTF, to NBP, to the supplier book, to the customer contract. September was the strongest month for Hormuz LNG shipments since the war began, but that improvement must not be assumed to continue, and a tanker was hit by multiple projectiles off Qatar on Wednesday evening with casualties reported. Switch365's internal offered board has already moved: Winter 2026 gas is up 13.43p to 198.13p/therm and Winter 2026 power is up £3.53 to £153.27/MWh this morning. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow.

Overnight in 30 seconds

  • Crude flow through Hormuz fell to about 10.1m bpd, down roughly 27% and about 74% of pre-war volumes.
  • Alternative Red Sea and Gulf of Oman routes have risen to about 6.7m bpd and are compensating for much of the loss.
  • A tanker was hit by multiple projectiles 51 nautical miles north of Qatar on Wednesday evening, with casualties reported (UKMTO).
  • The Houthis claim a fresh ballistic-missile attack on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport; Saudi confirmation of the latest claim was not available at the time of reporting.
  • Hurricane Isaias has shut in about 25% of US Gulf oil and 16% of US Gulf gas production ahead of a forecast Saturday landfall.

What it means for UK energy

The attacks are now beginning to show up in physical traffic, and European gas has responded: TTF and NBP public indications are up around 2.8% this morning and Switch365's offered Winter 2026 gas has jumped 13.43p in a day. The bearish buffers (alternative crude routes, Norway, US LNG, storage injections, IEA oil releases) are holding, so this is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market: customers with valid quotes or 2027-2030 renewals should check today's pricing before supplier books catch up with the wholesale move.

Section 05

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their contract ends to see today's price for future delivery: the offered ranges in Sections 3 and 4 are live, transactable indications for delivery seasons stretching out to 2030.

Backwardation is the market condition visible on this morning's board: the front period (Winter 2026) is expensive because the war risk is happening now, while later delivery periods are priced far lower. Backwardation does not mean wait. Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later; it means today's market currently prices that future delivery period more cheaply.

The customer mistake is assuming a cheap future period will still be cheap when they eventually get there. If Hormuz disruption spreads into Qatar LNG, if a cold winter meets 73% storage, or if another escalation hits, those 2028-2030 prices reprice upwards long before delivery begins. Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity.

Waiting is not neutral. Waiting only works if the market moves in the customer's favour.

Section 08 | Switch365 internal evidence

British Gas 18-Month Window

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing. The new agreement would not start today; it starts when the existing contract ends.

The 18-month window matters because it can make today's cheaper forward delivery periods commercially accessible. On this morning's board, S-27 gas is offered 27.1% below W-26 and W-27 gas 30.6% below; on power, S-27 is 26.3% and W-27 25.9% below the front. A customer whose contract end date sits up to 18 months away can now test those seasons instead of waiting for the traditional 12-month window.

If the market or supplier book changes, customers cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action: check eligibility, check the live board, and compare against the customer's actual contract end date.

Not every customer is automatically eligible. Eligibility and live pricing should be confirmed on a case-by-case basis.

Section 10

Top Five Warning Signs

1

Qatar LNG transits fall

The most direct Hormuz-to-UK gas consequence. September's improvement (19 shipments, the strongest of the war) must not be assumed to continue. Daily checks on Ras Laffan loadings, dark transits and vessels emerging outside Hormuz are now the single most important watch item.

2

Hormuz crude and vessel traffic fall further

Seven tracked transits on 6 October became about ten on 7 October. If the count falls back to single digits and stays there, the current deterioration becomes sustained and the crude premium rebuilds.

3

Multiple vessels are routinely turned back

The IRGC hailing of an inbound tanker near Khasab, ordering it to turn back or be targeted, was direct commercial navigation interference. If that becomes routine, interference becomes operational restriction.

4

US Gulf hurricane disrupts LNG export terminals

Isaias has already shut in about 25% of Gulf oil and 16% of Gulf gas production. The current track skirts most LNG terminals, but port restrictions, feedgas losses or a track change would weaken one of Europe's biggest alternative supplies.

5

NBP / TTF rise and suppliers pull books

The direct customer consequence. Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew pricing, temporarily suspended books and returned at higher levels. The public NBP indication is already near 199p.

Section 17 | Switch365 internal commercial experience

Supplier Price-Book Lag

Wholesale has moved. Have supplier books caught up?

Wholesale market moves→ Supplier book temporarily unchanged→ Hedging cost catches up→ Supplier reprices or withdraws→ Customer loses old rate

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. This morning the public NBP indication rose about 2.8% towards 199p/therm and Switch365's W-26 offered gas jumped 13.43p. Switch365 internal experience: when wholesale gas previously reached around 180p/therm, some suppliers withdrew pricing, temporarily suspended books and returned at higher levels. That is internal commercial experience, not an official industry threshold, but the mechanism is well established.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. The opportunity: a quote produced before the latest wholesale move may still be valid and may now sit materially below replacement cost. The danger: once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.

A 13.43p overnight move in the W-26 offered benchmark is exactly the kind of move that has historically triggered book reviews. Every valid quote on file should be re-verified today.
