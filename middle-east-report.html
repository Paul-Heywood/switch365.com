Section 1

Summary

Donald Trump has denied reports that he offered Iran sanctions relief and the release of frozen funds, writing on Truth Social: “This is untrue. I offered them NOTHING.” The denial came hours after the US imposed fresh sanctions on 13 individuals and entities across Russia, China, Hong Kong, Pakistan and Iran accused of supporting Iranian weapons procurement, part of the administration's Operation Economic Outcast campaign. Qatari shuttle diplomacy continues, with Doha “exchanging messages between the parties”, but no verified agreement has been reached on reopening Hormuz, on sanctions relief, on nuclear terms or on a ceasefire. This is the freshest direct duration development: the most credible diplomatic route to normalisation is still open, but the terms are hardening on both sides.

At the same time, the final US forces have completed their withdrawal from Iraqi bases under the timetable agreed by Washington and Baghdad in 2024, with most personnel repositioned to Jordan and other regional locations rather than sent home. Iran and the IRGC are presenting the departure as a strategic victory for the “axis of resistance”, and an Iran-backed militia commander called it “a historic victory for the Iraqi Islamic resistance groups”. Iraqi security experts warn the move could expand Tehran's influence and reduce US intelligence, surveillance and air-defence support inside Iraq. No immediate Iraqi oil production or export loss has been verified: this is a strategic duration signal, not an automatic energy-supply shock.

The physical counterweight is real. Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline restarted on 22 September, Yanbu crude and refined-product loadings have resumed, and Middle Eastern crude exports recovered to approximately 16.328 million b/d in September, the highest since the conflict began, though still below the pre-war February level of roughly 19.5 million b/d. Visible Qatar-linked LNG transits through Hormuz have increased from near zero in August, and European gas has softened from its September highs, with front-month TTF easing towards €69/MWh late on 29 September. None of that removes duration risk, and customer pricing still requires live NBP, TTF and supplier-book evidence rather than the Iraq headline or Brent alone.

Section 5

Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period.

A customer does not have to wait until their contract end date to see today's price for future delivery.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.

Backwardation does not mean wait.

Backwardation means nearer delivery is priced above later delivery. Today's internal curves show exactly that beyond the front seasons: gas S-27 at 129.00 p/therm against S-30 at 67.86, and power W-26 at £103.50/MWh against S-29 at £74.57. That gap can create an opportunity for future-start procurement, but it does not guarantee those prices will remain available. The relevant question is: can the customer secure the cheaper future delivery today? Where supplier eligibility and procurement rules allow, a future-start contract can be secured now.

Section 8

British Gas 18-Month Window

British Gas may allow an eligible customer to secure a future-start contract up to 18 months before their current contract ends. The new contract starts after the existing contract finishes, not today.

Eligibility must be checked: not every customer qualifies automatically, and supplier availability, term and credit remain relevant. The price is not protected until the contract is accepted and confirmed.

In today's backwardated market this window matters. A customer inside 18 months of their contract end date may be able to secure a future-start price while later delivery is offered materially below the front seasons, for example S-30 gas at 67.86 p/therm against the S-27 front at 129.00, or S-29 power at £74.57/MWh against W-26 at £103.50. Supplier volatility and duration risk cut both ways: the same diplomatic hardening that keeps the front elevated could push books higher, and once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action.
