● Futures Market Explained

The futures market shows what the wholesale market is currently asking for energy delivered in future periods. A business whose contract ends in 2027, 2028, 2029 or 2030 should therefore look at the market price for its actual future delivery period, not just today's near-term price.

When future periods are materially cheaper than the front of the curve, the market is described as backwardated. The gas and power tables above show this clearly: S-27, W-27, S-28 and beyond are all priced well below W-26.

Backwardation does not mean wait. The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period. As time passes, that future period becomes the near-term market and can absorb the same geopolitical, supply and demand risk premium.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. The % vs W-26 column shows how much cheaper or more expensive each future delivery period is compared with the latest W-26 price. A negative percentage does not mean energy will be cheaper later. It means that future delivery period is currently cheaper than the front of the curve.

● Supplier Price-Book Lag

Wholesale markets can move before every supplier reprices its customer-facing book. Wholesale moved first. Supplier books may still follow.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. A supplier may still have rates available that have not fully absorbed the latest wholesale move. Once the supplier reprices, yesterday's available quote may disappear.

If live supplier pricing still reflects the earlier market, that creates a window to review. It is not a guarantee that pricing will remain available. This is a review-now window, not a wait-and-see market.
