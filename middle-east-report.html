Iran: "Era of Proportionate Responses Is Over" as Commercial Tankers Enter the Firing Line

Iran says future retaliation will be faster, heavier and more painful after direct US and Iranian attacks involving oil tankers over the weekend. Hormuz traffic has fallen to its lowest sustained level since May, Tehran is preparing a new restricted zone and the Houthi conflict is simultaneously raising risk around Bab al-Mandab. UK gas has opened the week higher and the internal supplier book has now repriced with it: Monday's offered range puts W-26 gas at 183.00p/therm and W-26 power at 150.00 £/MWh.

Research window: Friday 4 September to Monday 7 September 2026 (Europe/London). Internal pricing snapshots: Friday 4 September AM versus Monday 7 September AM. Public market data timestamped Monday 7 September 2026, morning run.

01Summary

What changed over the weekend that could change the price a UK business can actually secure today.

Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf declared on Sunday that "the era of proportionate responses has come to an end", warning that any further attack on Iranian interests will receive a "faster, heavier and more painful" response and that recent strikes were "only the beginning". Iran is signalling that it no longer intends to constrain future retaliation to what it considers proportionate responses. That raises the escalation tail-risk around every subsequent US-Iran exchange, though it does not by itself prove that the next strike will be larger or that Hormuz will close completely.

The statement matters because of what happened before it. On Saturday, US Central Command said US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers, one of them off Kharg Island, Iran's principal crude export hub, after the IRGC launched ballistic missiles at two US Navy warships (both evaded, no US personnel harmed). The IRGC Navy said it retaliated by targeting three tankers on routes Tehran considered unauthorised, and claimed further attacks on US-linked vessels that Washington has not confirmed. Maritime intelligence firm Marisks described the weekend as a major escalation because the distinction between military confrontation and commercial shipping has materially weakened. Kpler data shows only two commodity vessels transited Hormuz on Saturday and six on Sunday, with the 10-day average down to ten per day, the lowest since May, and no VLCC has exited the Strait since Wednesday.

UK gas answered the weekend on Monday morning, and so did the supplier book. Public NBP traded above 183 pence per therm, its highest level in more than three and a half years and around 2.7% above Friday's reference, and the internal Monday AM offered range confirms the repricing: W-26 gas is up 4.50p to 183.00p/therm and W-26 power is up 4.00 to 150.00 £/MWh against Friday morning. Wholesale moved first and supplier books have followed. Any Friday quote still being honoured this morning is now a pre-escalation price sitting below the current book.

The Weekend in 30 Seconds

  • Iran says the era of proportionate responses is over and threatens faster, heavier and more painful retaliation. Confirmed statement
  • The US struck three Iranian oil tankers on Saturday (Downy, Stark I and Kylo/Noxen, per maritime intelligence); Iran says it targeted three tankers using unauthorised Hormuz routes. US and Iranian claims remain separately attributed.
  • Hormuz traffic fell to two vessels on Saturday and six on Sunday; the 10-day average of ten per day is the lowest since May. No VLCC has exited since Wednesday.
  • Iran says a new restricted zone beyond the Strait will be announced in the coming days, with entering vessels added to its sanctions list; the blacklist already stands at 56 vessels after 11 were added.
  • Yemen's Houthi conflict intensified sharply towards Mokha and the approaches to Bab al-Mandab, with more than 300 reported killed since Thursday (military and medical source tallies, not independently verified).
  • Brent is around $97 per barrel (roughly +10% last week), public NBP is above 183p/therm, and Monday's internal offered range has repriced higher across the board: gas W-26 +4.50p, power W-26 +4.00 £/MWh.

What It Means for UK Energy

Oil headlines are dramatic, but LNG remains the bridge between Hormuz and UK gas. Qatar LNG is still severely constrained (18 cargoes exported during the comparable conflict period versus 509 a year earlier, roughly a 96% reduction), European storage is only around 62% full and below the seasonal norm, and winter is approaching. Europe has less room to absorb another physical shock. For UK businesses the transmission runs Hormuz, Qatar LNG, global LNG competition, TTF, NBP, gas-fired power, supplier price books, customer contract. The weekend did not produce a resolution; it produced more direct maritime escalation, and Monday's internal book shows that escalation is now inside the price a customer can actually secure.

05Futures Market Explained

Why a customer can see, and buy, future delivery periods today.

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their contract end date to see today's price for future delivery.

Near-term gas remains extremely expensive relative to many later delivery periods: latest W-26 gas is 183.00p/therm against 120.50p for S-27, 117.50p for W-27, 79.00p for S-28, 86.50p for W-28 and 65.48p for S-30. Do not let 180p-plus near-term gas create the impression that all future gas is 180p-plus.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. The customer mistake is thinking a cheaper future period means they should wait. It does not. It means we should check whether that future period can be secured now. Backwardation does not mean wait. The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period, and waiting gives the market time to remove today's forward-value opportunity. Waiting is not neutral.

08British Gas 18-Month Window

Switch365 internal commercial evidence: eligibility and mechanics.

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier chance to review future-start pricing. The 18-month British Gas window matters because it turns the forward market into a practical customer action: British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action.

Your new contract would not start today. It would begin when your current agreement ends. Not every customer is automatically eligible, so the first step is an eligibility and live-pricing check.

In today's market the window connects directly to the curve: W-27 gas is priced 35.8% below W-26 and S-28 56.8% below it, while the front of the market has just repriced 4.50p higher on the weekend escalation. A customer 12 to 18 months from renewal can now test pricing for a delivery period the market currently values far below the distressed front, before further sessions like this morning's work their way down the curve.

Waiting is not neutral. If the market moves or British Gas withdraws rates, customers cannot go back and secure today's pricing later. Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. It means check whether the future period can be secured now.

10Top Five Warning Signs

Exactly five, ranked by potential UK price impact.

  • 1. Iran implements the new restricted zone. Especially if commercial ships are turned back, detained or added to the sanctions list; the zone could effectively expand the blacklist system well beyond the named 56 vessels. Announced, not yet operational.
  • 2. Another tanker is attacked, especially an LNG carrier. Saturday moved commercial shipping from bystander to direct object of the confrontation; an LNG strike would hit the UK gas transmission chain directly.
  • 3. Iran follows through on "heavier" retaliation. Watch energy assets, US bases and shipping. Physical action is the reality check for the new doctrine.
  • 4. Houthis move closer to Bab al-Mandab or Mokha. Especially if merchant shipping reacts, insurance rises or traffic reroutes; Mokha becoming cut off would upgrade the Bab al-Mandab story.
  • 5. NBP, TTF or supplier books reprice sharply again. NBP is already above 183p and the internal book has moved once today; a second repricing this week would compound the front-winter increase and start closing the forward-value gap further out.

The energy risk is no longer concentrated around one Middle East chokepoint: if Hormuz is the problem, Bab al-Mandab is part of the workaround. A route can remain physically open and still carry a material war-risk premium. Workarounds reduce outright shortage risk, but increase time, cost and complexity.

The question was never just where wholesale is; it is which supplier books have caught up. This morning the internal evidence answers it: the offered range moved through the 180p/therm internal repricing warning level and came back higher, consistent with Switch365 experience that some suppliers withdraw books, temporarily suspend rates or return with higher prices when wholesale gas reaches that area (internal commercial evidence; 180p is a useful warning level, not an official supplier threshold). Customers holding an unsecured Friday quote are now looking at a front-winter offer 4.50p/therm higher on gas and 4.00 £/MWh higher on power than they were on Friday morning.

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger. Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow, and today they followed within one session. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price. The market does not need to rise again tomorrow for customers to pay more tomorrow.
Price accuracy: a wholesale move does not translate pound-for-pound into the customer's total energy bill. Wholesale energy is only one component of customer pricing, alongside network charges, policy costs and supplier margin. Never read a 10% Brent move as a 10% bill move: Brent is not the customer's gas quote.
