Futures Market Explained
How forward pricing works for UK business energy customers

What the futures market actually is

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A 2028 contract is not priced using today's Day-Ahead market. If 2028 is materially cheaper than W-26 today, that is a current forward-market opportunity. It does not mean the customer should wait until 2028.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. Backwardation does not mean wait. Waiting allows today's future period to become tomorrow's near-term period. The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period.

The % vs W-26 comparison is the clearest measure of the current forward-value gap. If future periods remain significantly below W-26, customers should test whether they can secure them now. Geopolitical escalation matters because forward prices can move before a customer's contract starts.

Key rules for reading the curve

1. W-26 is the benchmark front
All % vs W-26 calculations use the latest W-26 price as the denominator.
2. Negative % means cheaper than front
A negative percentage means that future delivery period is currently cheaper than W-26. It does not predict a price fall.
3. 1 Year Ago is context only
Use the 1 Year Ago line as context, not as the customer's available price. Today's latest price is the decision point.
4. Waiting is not neutral
If the market moves or a supplier withdraws today's rates, customers cannot go back later and secure the same pricing.

British Gas 18-Month Window
Verified Switch365 internal evidence

What changed at British Gas

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing.

The 18-month British Gas window matters because it turns the forward market into practical customer action. Your new contract would not start today. It would begin when your current agreement ends.

Waiting is not neutral. If the market moves or British Gas withdraws today's rates, customers cannot go back later and secure the same pricing. Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later. It means check whether that future delivery period can be secured now.

PRACTICAL EXAMPLE
If your current British Gas contract ends in March 2028, the 18-month window means you may be able to review and secure pricing for that future period today.
S-28 gas is currently offered at 71.00p/therm. W-26 is 154.25p/therm. That is a 54.0% discount to the front of the curve.
But that 71.00p price is only available while suppliers are still quoting it. If the market reprices on duration risk, that gap can close before your contract start date arrives.

Top Five Warning Signs
Ranked by commercial impact on UK business energy
1

Iran Shifts Towards Offence

A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran is shifting from defence to a fully offensive military posture. The IRGC political deputy confirmed the armed forces will adopt a more "offensive form" of defence. This is a duration and military-escalation signal. If the shift becomes operational policy, energy markets must price a greater probability of continued attacks on shipping and regional infrastructure.

2

60-Day Negotiation Window Expires

The June MoU's 60-day negotiating window expired on 17 August without a final settlement and without a US extension. President Trump said the US is not seeking an extension. That does not prove diplomacy is over, but it removes one of the clearest formal routes towards a quick settlement. Backchannels may remain active, but formal settlement and backchannel communication are not the same thing.

3

Shipping Attacks Continue

On Tuesday morning UKMTO confirmed a vessel was struck by an unknown projectile while exiting Hormuz, causing engine room damage and a crew casualty. Attribution must be verified before stating who was responsible. Another shipping incident reinforces the behavioural risk. Shipowners, crews and insurers react to repeated incidents, not only confirmed route closures.

4

No Tracked LNG Tankers or VLCCs Through Hormuz Monday

Latest Kpler data shows only six commodity vessels crossed Hormuz on Monday. No tracked LNG tanker. No tracked VLCC. The composition of traffic matters as much as the headline vessel count. A few smaller product or LPG vessels crossing does not mean oil and LNG export flows have normalised. LNG is the bridge between Hormuz and UK gas.

5

Qatar / Red Sea Alternatives Remain Constrained

Qatar LNG force majeure remains unresolved with no definite September plan confirmed. Asian and European buyers are competing for replacement LNG. Meanwhile Bab el-Mandeb remains operational but risky, with Monday traffic at 19 vessels against a 10-day average of 26. Theoretical export capacity is not the same as practical, risk-free export capacity.

Supplier Price-Book Lag
Why this is both the opportunity and the danger

Supplier price-book lag is the opportunity and the danger.

Wholesale markets move first. Supplier books often follow. Waiting for certainty can mean missing the price movement. Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.

Suppliers do not need a full Hormuz closure before repricing. They do not need every Qatari LNG cargo to disappear. They do not need the conflict to be officially forecast to last years. They price risk, replacement cost and probability.

The combination of an expired diplomatic framework, Iranian escalation rhetoric, continued attacks and missing LNG tanker traffic is precisely the type of information that can feed into supplier risk models.

WHAT SUPPLIERS DO NOT NEED TO WAIT FOR
— A complete Hormuz closure — Qatar LNG to stop entirely — A direct UK supply shortage — A 2029 war forecast — A formal Iranian declaration of long-term war
WHAT SUPPLIERS CAN REACT TO NOW
— Higher wholesale prices — LNG replacement costs — Shipping insurance and freight — Volatility and collateral requirements — Geopolitical risk and declining liquidity — Probability of future disruption
