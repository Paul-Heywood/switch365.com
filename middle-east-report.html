05 · Futures market explained

Future delivery is priced today

A commercial explanation, not a direction-of-market forecast.

What the curve means

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their existing contract ends to see today's future-delivery price.

It means future delivery is cheaper today.

Backwardation describes the shape of the offered curve. It does not guarantee that waiting will produce a lower quote, nor does it establish the customer's full bill, which also depends on standing charges, non-commodity costs, consumption and product structure.

What the curve does not mean

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.
Backwardation does not mean wait.

Waiting gives the market time to remove today's opportunity. Waiting is not neutral. The correct action is to test a real, eligible future-start offer against the customer's contract end date and risk requirements.

08 · Switch365 internal commercial evidence

British Gas 18-Month Window

Eligibility turns forward-market intelligence into a practical customer action.

What changed for eligible customers

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier opportunity to review future-start pricing.

The contract does not start today. It begins when the existing agreement ends. The 18-month window turns today's forward curve into something customers may actually be able to secure.

British Gas 18-month eligibility turns this market intelligence into a practical customer action.

What must be checked before securing

  • Confirm that the specific customer, site, product and contract are eligible.
  • Obtain a current supplier quote and record its expiry and terms.
  • Check that the future start aligns with the existing contract end date.
  • Compare unit rates, standing charges, pass-through items and consumption.
  • Do not assume every British Gas customer qualifies automatically.

Once a supplier withdraws a rate, the customer cannot go back and secure yesterday's price.
