The important change is duration. The market does not need another major military attack to rise; it only needs confidence in a quick resolution to weaken. The dip customers saw earlier this week was a diplomatic-relief move, not a new lower trend, and part of it has already been given back. This is a review-now market, not a blindly-sign-now market.

Futures Market Explained

What the forward curve is, and how to read it as a customer.

The futures market shows today's price for energy delivered in a future period. A customer does not have to wait until their contract end date to see today's price for future delivery: that price exists now, and in many cases it can be secured now through a future-start contract. When the front of the curve sits far above later delivery periods, the market is in backwardation: it is pricing a problem it expects to ease, not a price it promises will be available later.

  • “Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.” A lower price for a 2028 delivery period is today's price for that period, not a forecast of where the market will be in 2028.
  • “The customer mistake is thinking a cheaper future period means they should wait. It does not. It means we should check whether that future period can be secured now.”
  • “Backwardation does not mean wait.” Backwardation is the shape of today's curve, not a schedule for future bargains.
  • “The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period.”

British Gas 18-Month Window

Internal verified Switch365 evidence.

British Gas has extended its business energy renewal window from 12 months to 18 months. This gives eligible customers an earlier chance to review future-start pricing.

  • The 18-month British Gas window matters because it turns the forward market into a practical customer action. The far curve shown above is not theoretical for eligible customers: it is a delivery period they can test for live pricing today.
  • Your new contract would not start today. It would begin when your current agreement ends. Nothing about the current supply arrangement changes; the decision is about the price of the next period.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

One of the most commercially important mechanics in this market.

Supplier price books do not update tick-by-tick. The sequence runs like this: geopolitical optimism rises, wholesale falls, supplier pricing may improve with a lag. Then geopolitical confidence deteriorates, wholesale rebounds, and supplier books reprice - usually faster on the way up than they eased on the way down. This week is a live demonstration: the diplomatic dip softened wholesale on Monday-Wednesday, and before supplier books could fully pass that softness through, Thursday's rebound took W-26 gas back up 5.25p on the internal board.
