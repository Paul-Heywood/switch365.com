Yesterday the pause cracked. This morning the crack is in the prices.

Tuesday's escalation has now moved straight through to wholesale. The internal offered curve supplied this morning has snapped back: gas W-26 is up 5.50 p/th to 142.50 — giving back almost all of Tuesday's 6.25 p/th relief fall in one session — and power W-26 is up £3.50 to £121.50/MWh. Customers should not read oil off the highs as cheaper business energy. The energy bill conversation is about NBP, TTF, UK power, supplier books, contract end date and forward delivery period. Supplier price-book lag is still the opportunity and the danger — but it has now flipped direction: books are repricing up off Tuesday night's news, and quotes generated this morning are perishable.

Futures Market Explained

Why the delivery period matters more than today’s headline price.

The futures market shows the price for energy delivered in future periods. A customer renewing from 2027, 2028, 2029 or 2030 is not only interested in today’s price. They should look at what the market is currently offering for their actual delivery period.

The curve is in deepening backwardation: the front (W-26) carries the war-risk premium from the Jordan interception, the Iraq strikes and the unresolved Hormuz impasse, while later delivery periods are offered at large discounts — and that discount grew again today even as the front rose. Gas S-29 is now -56.8% vs W-26; power S-29 -47.6%.

Cheaper further out does not mean cheaper later.
The future market is only useful while the customer can still buy that future delivery period.
Waiting lets the market make the decision for the customer.
As time passes, that future period becomes the near-term market and can absorb the same risk premium.

The practical point for every conversation today: a cheaper 2028 or 2029 price is not a forecast of what the customer will pay if they wait. It is a live offer that exists now — and today's curve shows exactly how it dies: the front moves first, then the far curve follows. Gas W-29 rose 1.87 and S-30 rose 1.29 overnight. The back of the curve is already starting to catch up.

Market Drivers

The verified forces moving UK gas, UK power and oil today.

1 · Jordan interception — the pause cracked 28–29 Jul

US Central Command said it intercepted multiple Iranian ballistic missiles launched towards US forces in the Middle East in an “attempted surprise attack”. The IRGC said it fired several ballistic missiles at a US air base and a US CENTCOM centre in Jordan, cast as retaliation for US attacks on Iran's interests. Jordanian state media said Jordan shot down five missiles coming from Iran. No US casualties were reported.

2 · US / Saudi strikes in Iraq 29 Jul

CENTCOM and the Saudi armed forces struck what Washington called Iran-backed militant logistics and weapons sites across eastern Iraq, in response to “over 30 IRGC-directed aerial drone attacks in the last 72 hours”. It is the first time Riyadh has taken responsibility for strikes inside Iraq since the war began; Iraq's Popular Mobilisation Forces said its bases were hit in the “deadly” attacks and reported several killed and wounded. A specific death toll remains unverified.

3 · Saudi drone interceptions & denials 27–29 Jul

Saudi Arabia said it intercepted and destroyed drones targeting Eastern Province oil facilities — and Riyadh — for a second consecutive day, blaming Iran-backed groups operating from Iraqi airspace. The Houthis separately claimed drone strikes on Saudi oil infrastructure; IRGC-affiliated media claimed a fire at Abqaiq, which Saudi officials have not confirmed. Iran denied involvement and said attributing the attacks to Tehran was a “major miscalculation”.

4 · Hormuz impasse hardens 29 Jul

Iran rejected Oman's proposal for shared 50-50 control of the Strait of Hormuz, insisting Tehran must retain full control of the inbound shipping lane and part of the outbound route — and accusing the US and Saudi Arabia of pressuring Oman into “unrealistic plans”. This hardens the position from Monday's “significant progress” mediation headlines. The IRGC claim to have struck and stopped three oil tankers remains unverified.

5 · Red Sea second chokepoint 27–30 Jul

The Houthi blockade of Saudi ports and ships in Bab el-Mandeb, declared on 20 July, remains in force. The Houthis claimed a missile attack on the Saudi tanker NCC Ghazal, saying it was forced to turn back; UKMTO reported a tanker hearing explosions in the southern Red Sea with crew safe. Saudi crude continues to reroute via Egypt's Sidi Kerir (SUMED), and Bab el-Mandeb tonnage is running at roughly half normal levels.

6 · Oil, stocks and the money 21–29 Jul

Brent settled at $86.89 on 29 July (+3.3%) after surging past $88 intraday, snapping the three-session relief slide; API data showed US crude inventories down 3.3 million barrels, pointing to continued tightness. US gasoline is $4.11/gal. EU-27 gas storage at 57.7 Bcm is 12.0 Bcm below last year. Hegseth put the war's cost at $37.5bn so far, defending an $87.6bn supplemental request inside a wider ~$95bn package.

Kazakhstan / CPC / Black Sea: no fresh verified development in this research window — it remains a secondary supply-route issue and has dropped down the priority list. No fresh NESO Electricity Margin Notice or Gassco Norwegian outage was verified in this window.

Shipping Risk

Two chokepoints under pressure at the same time — the physical backbone of the price story.

Strait of Hormuz Unresolved

Status: legally open, effectively constrained — and the politics hardened this week. Iran rejected shared 50-50 control and demands full control of the inbound lane plus part of the outbound; the IMO has urged member states to reject Iranian efforts to impose sovereignty over the strait.

Traffic: ~19 transits on 28 July versus 63 on 3 July. Lloyd's List Intelligence's 21 July brief quantifies the damage: total traffic down ~90% year-on-year, non-Iranian-linked transits down to 25 in a week from 108, inbound traffic down to 8 vessels, and nearly 70% of tanker transits running dark (AIS off). Wartime flows remain a fraction of the norm on a waterway that carries about a fifth of the world's traded oil and ~20% of global LNG.

Claims vs fact: the IRGC says it “struck and stopped” three oil tankers on 29 July after they ignored warnings. Not independently verified in this research run — treat as a claim. Documented July attacks on tankers (7, 12, 14, 20, 21 July) stand separately.

Bab el-Mandeb / Red Sea Deteriorating

Status: the Houthi blockade of Saudi ports and ships, declared 20 July, is in force. The Houthis claimed a missile attack on the Saudi tanker NCC Ghazal, saying it was forced to turn back; UKMTO reported a tanker hearing explosions in the southern Red Sea; crew and vessel safe.

Impact: Bab el-Mandeb tonnage down ~50%; Saudi crude being rerouted via Egypt's Sidi Kerir terminal (SUMED pipeline) with AIS suppression reported at Yanbu loadings; at least one Indian refiner (MRPL) tendering on explicit avoidance of both the Red Sea and Hormuz.

Why it matters for the UK: Suez/SUMED and Yanbu are the relief valves for Gulf supply that cannot use Hormuz. If both chokepoints are constrained at once, freight, insurance and delivery-risk premia feed straight into the oil and LNG complex that sets the marginal UK gas and power price — which is exactly what this morning's offered curve is showing.

Shipping confidence: low and falling. Operators are going dark through Hormuz, insurance “remains available throughout” only at elevated cost, and prediction markets put the odds of Hormuz traffic returning to normal by 31 July at under 1%. There is no verified return to normal scheduling by any mainline carrier in this research window.

Supplier Price-Book Lag

Why the gap between wholesale and quoted rates is today’s biggest opportunity — and biggest danger.
Wholesale moved first. Supplier books are now following — upwards.

Supplier price books do not reprint in real time. Tuesday morning's relief pricing pulled wholesale offers down; Tuesday night's escalation pushed them straight back up — internal gas W-26 +5.50 p/th and power W-26 +£3.50/MWh on Wednesday morning. Supplier price-book lag is still the opportunity and the danger, and today it runs in the customer's disfavour the longer they wait.

The opportunity: any book still carrying Tuesday's relief assumptions is temporarily cheaper than the market it will become. And the forward curve remains deeply backwardated — gas S-28 to S-30 at -51.8% to -56.8% vs W-26, power S-28 / S-29 at -46.5% to -47.6%. Customers quoted now can still catch that gap.

The danger: desks are repricing, quote validity is shortening, and the far curve itself is starting to rise (gas W-29 +1.87, S-30 +1.29 overnight). Once suppliers withdraw rates, customers cannot go back and secure previous pricing. The 2028–2030 discount is not a permanent feature — it erodes from both ends.

Operating rule for today: any quote generated this morning is perishable. Get decisions inside the validity window, and do not promise customers that this morning's prices will still exist tomorrow.
